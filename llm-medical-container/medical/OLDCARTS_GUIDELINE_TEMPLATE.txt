# OLDCARTS-Structured Clinical Guideline Template

## Classic Presentation Format (Concise)

ONSET: [Sudden/Gradual] onset, typically within [timeframe]. [Acute/chronic context].

LOCATION: [Specific anatomical location]. Pain localizes to [precise area]. May radiate to [radiation pattern].

DURATION: Episodes last [timeframe]. [Constant vs episodic].

CHARACTER: Pain is [quality] - [descriptors]. Typically [severity]/10 severity. NOT [what it's NOT - key negative].

AGGRAVATING: Pain WORSENS WITH [triggers/activities]. [Specific aggravators].

RELIEVING: Pain IMPROVES WITH [relieving factors] or NOTHING HELPS [if applicable].

TIMING: Pain is [CONSTANT/INTERMITTENT/EPISODIC]. [Pattern details].

SEVERITY: Typically [range]/10. [Mild/Moderate/Severe descriptor].

ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: [Key symptoms in order of importance]. [Specific findings].

MIGRATION: [Does it migrate? Pattern if yes]. MIGRATION from [A] to [B] is HIGHLY SPECIFIC for [condition].

KEY POSITIVES: [Pathognomonic findings]. [High-yield features].

KEY NEGATIVES: [Findings that argue against this diagnosis]. [Alternative diagnoses if these present].

## Structured OLDCARTS Format (NEW)

Each OLDCARTS element now includes both medical terms and patient-friendly terms.

**IMPORTANT - Simplification Principle:**
- **Timing**: Include ONLY the PRIMARY timing pattern for this condition (constant, intermittent, OR episodic - not all). The synonyms file handles variations (e.g., "continuous" → "constant", "comes and goes" → "intermittent").
- **Duration**: Include only timeframes relevant to this condition (seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks - not all).
- **Order**: onset, location, timing, duration, character, aggravating, relieving, severity

## Patient-Friendly Term Guidelines

- Use simple, everyday language
- Avoid medical jargon
- Use "you" and "your" for personal connection
- Keep anatomical descriptions clear and relatable
- Use common body part references (belly, ribs, groin, etc.)
- Make pain descriptions relatable (sharp, dull, achy, etc.)

## Example: Acute Appendicitis (OLDCARTS-Structured)

ONSET: SUDDEN onset, typically within 6-48 hours. Acute presentation (NOT chronic - >5 days suggests alternative).

LOCATION: Pain MIGRATES from periumbilical to right lower quadrant (RLQ) over 12-24 hours. Localizes to McBurney's point in RLQ.

DURATION: CONSTANT pain since onset. Lasts hours to days (not episodic).

CHARACTER: SHARP and CONSTANT (NOT crampy or intermittent). Steady, unrelenting quality.

AGGRAVATING: WORSENS WITH MOVEMENT - can't jump, cough, or tolerate car bumps. Movement dramatically intensifies pain.

RELIEVING: Lying still provides slight relief. NOTHING fully relieves pain. Position changes worsen it.

TIMING: CONSTANT since onset. Progressively worsens over time (not waves or episodes).

SEVERITY: 7-10/10. Moderate to severe pain that interferes with all activities.

ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: ANOREXIA (>90% - ask "are you hungry?"), nausea and vomiting (AFTER pain onset), low-grade fever (100-102°F). Constipation common, minimal diarrhea.

MIGRATION: HIGHLY SPECIFIC - periumbilical → RLQ migration over 12-24 hours.

KEY POSITIVES: Anorexia, RLQ localization, migration pattern, pain with movement.

KEY NEGATIVES: Pain starting in RLQ without periumbilical origin is less typical. CRAMPY pain suggests obstruction. High fever >103°F suggests perforation. PROFUSE WATERY DIARRHEA suggests gastroenteritis.
