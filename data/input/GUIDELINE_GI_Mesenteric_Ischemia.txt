Condition: Acute Mesenteric Ischemia
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: emergent
Prevalence: rare
ICD-10 Code: K55.059
SNOMED Code: 61490001
Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • severe abdominal pain
  • stomach pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within hours. Acute, dramatic presentation. LOCATION: Diffuse, PERIUMBILICAL. Not localized to one quadrant. DURATION: CONSTANT from onset. Pain persists continuously without relief. CHARACTER: SEVERE, CONSTANT pain initially crampy/colicky, then becomes steady. Described as deep, severe ache. AGGRAVATING: Eating may worsen (post-prandial angina pattern). Movement doesn't significantly change (pain internal). RELIEVING: NOTHING helps. Pain is UNRELENTING despite narcotics. Requires vascular intervention for relief. TIMING: CONSTANT and unrelenting. No wave pattern. Progressively worsens. SEVERITY: 9-10/10, PAIN OUT OF PROPORTION to exam findings (severe pain but minimal tenderness on exam - hallmark). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: BLOODY DIARRHEA (late finding), nausea, vomiting, urgent bowel movements. Patient in severe distress despite soft abdomen. KEY POSITIVES: Classic triad: severe pain + minimal exam findings + elderly (>60) with vascular disease (AFib, PVD, prior myocardial infarction ). Metabolic acidosis. KEY NEGATIVES: Young patient WITHOUT vascular disease makes extremely unlikely. Severe tenderness on exam suggests alternative.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, acute, hours, dramatic
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: diffuse, periumbilical, central
  Negative indicators: localized, one quadrant, specific point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: constant, persists, continuously, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe, constant, crampy, colicky, steady, deep ache
  Negative indicators: mild, intermittent, sharp

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: eating, post-prandial, food
  Negative indicators: movement, rest, lying still

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: nothing, unrelenting, no relief
  Negative indicators: narcotics, medication, position change

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant, unrelenting, progressively worsens, no waves
  Negative indicators: wave pattern, intermittent, episodic

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 9, 8, 9-10/10, 10, severe, out of proportion
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, 1-5/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 EMERGENCY CONDITION - severe pain out of proportion to exam in elderly with AFib - call 911 immediately

  🚨 Bloody diarrhea with severe pain - bowel infarction, needs immediate vascular surgery

  🚨 Peritoneal signs developing - bowel necrosis/perforation, fatal without emergency surgery

  🚨 Metabolic acidosis with elevated lactate - tissue ischemia, extremely poor prognosis without immediate intervention
