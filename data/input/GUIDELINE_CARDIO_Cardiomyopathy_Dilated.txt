--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • shortness of breath
  • fatigue
  • leg swelling
  • can't breathe lying flat

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Progressive over weeks to months, acute decompensation possible. LOCATION: Generalized dyspnea, bilateral lower extremity edema. DURATION: Progressive, chronic with acute worsening. CHARACTER: Dyspnea on exertion progressing to dyspnea at rest, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, fatigue. AGGRAVATING: Exertion, lying flat, salt intake. RELIEVING: Diuretics, sitting upright. TIMING: Progressive worsening. SEVERITY: Severe dyspnea. KEY POSITIVES: Bilateral leg edema, weight gain, orthopnea, PND, S3 gallop, cardiomegaly on chest X-ray (CXR), history of alcohol abuse or chemotherapy or viral illness. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT acute myocardial infarction (MI) presentation, NOT pleuritic.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: progressive over weeks to months, acute decompensation possible, gradual
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, minutes

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: generalized dyspnea, bilateral lower extremity edema
  Negative indicators: unilateral leg swelling, localized, one area only

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: progressive, chronic with acute worsening, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: dyspnea on exertion, progressing to dyspnea at rest, orthopnea, paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, fatigue
  Negative indicators: acute myocardial infarction presentation, pleuritic, sharp, stabbing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: exertion, lying flat, salt intake
  Negative indicators: diuretics, sitting upright, medication

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: diuretics, sitting upright
  Negative indicators: exertion, lying flat, salt intake

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: progressive worsening, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe dyspnea, severe
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, no symptoms

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea at rest

  🚨 Hypotension

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Cold extremities

  🚨 Severe edema or anasarca

  🚨 Arrhythmias (AFib, ventricular tachycardia )

  🚨 Syncope

  🚨 Chest pain (suggests ischemia)
