================================================================================

Condition: Acute Myocardial Infarction (Heart Attack)
Category: N/A

Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain
  • chest pressure
  • chest tightness
  • heart attack
  • crushing chest pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden or over minutes. LOCATION: RETROSTERNAL (behind breastbone), left-sided chest, or epigastric. RADIATES to left arm, jaw, neck, back, or both arms. DURATION: Continuous for >10-20 minutes, does NOT resolve with rest. CHARACTER: CRUSHING, pressure, squeezing, heaviness, "elephant on chest". NOT sharp or stabbing typically. AGGRAVATING: None (unprovoked, can occur at rest). RELIEVING: NOT relieved by position change, antacids, or nitroglycerin (if severe). TIMING: Persistent, progressive worsening. SEVERITY: 7-10/10, most severe pain patient has ever felt. KEY POSITIVES: Diaphoresis (sweating), nausea, vomiting, dyspnea (shortness of breath), sense of impending doom, pallor, weakness. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT sharp, NOT positional, NOT pleuritic (worse with breathing).

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, over minutes, acute
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, hours

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: retrosternal, behind breastbone, left-sided chest, epigastric, radiates to left arm, radiates to jaw, radiates to neck, radiates to back, radiates to both arms
  Negative indicators: right side only, no radiation, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: continuous, >10-20 minutes, does not resolve with rest, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes, resolves with rest

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: crushing, pressure, squeezing, heaviness, elephant on chest
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, burning, crampy

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: none, unprovoked, can occur at rest
  Negative indicators: movement, breathing, position

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: not relieved by position change, not relieved by antacids, not relieved by nitroglycerin
  Negative indicators: position change, antacids, nitroglycerin, rest

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: persistent, progressive worsening, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 7-10/10, most severe pain ever felt, 7, 8, 9, 10
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, 1-6/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Diaphoresis (profuse sweating)

  🚨 Shortness of breath or dyspnea

  🚨 Nausea or vomiting

  🚨 Radiation to left arm or jaw

  🚨 History of coronary artery disease

  🚨 Diabetes mellitus

  🚨 Hyperlipidemia or hypertension

  🚨 Family history of early myocardial infarction (MI)

  🚨 Syncope or near-syncope

  🚨 Altered mental status
