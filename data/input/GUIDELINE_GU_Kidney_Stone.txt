--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • flank pain
  • back pain
  • side pain
  • kidney pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within minutes to hours. Acute, dramatic presentation. LOCATION: Unilateral FLANK PAIN. RADIATES from flank→groin→testicle (males) or labia (females). Follows ureter path. DURATION: Waves last minutes, entire episode lasts hours. COLICKY (comes and goes in waves). CHARACTER: SEVERE COLICKY PAIN described as SHARP, STABBING, or 'WORST PAIN OF MY LIFE'. Intensity waxes and wanes. AGGRAVATING: NOTHING specifically worsens (spontaneous waves). Movement doesn't change intensity. Dehydration may precipitate. RELIEVING: NOTHING relieves during wave. Position changes don't help (patient RESTLESS, paces, cannot find comfort - unlike peritonitis where still). Narcotics needed. TIMING: COMES IN WAVES - intense for minutes, then slightly better, then intense again. Pattern repeats. Not constant. SEVERITY: 8-10/10, extremely severe and dramatic. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: NAUSEA AND VOMITING (very common), HEMATURIA (blood in urine - 90%, gross or microscopic), urinary frequency, urgency. KEY POSITIVES: cerebrovascular accident (CVA) tenderness, hematuria, colicky waves, restlessness, flank→groin radiation. KEY NEGATIVES: Peritoneal signs (rigidity) suggest alternative. FEVER suggests pyelonephritis not stone. Bilateral pain unusual.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Fever with flank pain - infected stone (pyonephrosis), needs urgent urology intervention

  🚨 Anuria (no urine output) - complete obstruction, needs urgent decompression

  🚨 Single kidney with obstructing stone - urgent urology consult

  🚨 Severe pain unresponsive to narcotics - may need urgent intervention
