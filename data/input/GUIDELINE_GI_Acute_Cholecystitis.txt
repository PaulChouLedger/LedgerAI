CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • right upper abdominal pain
  • right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain
  • upper right side pain
  • pain after eating
  • gallbladder pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within 12-48 hours, often 30 minutes to 2 hours AFTER A FATTY MEAL (fried foods, heavy meals). LOCATION: Right upper quadrant (RUQ), precisely localized just below right rib cage. RADIATES TO RIGHT SHOULDER OR SCAPULA (phrenic nerve referred pain). DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting >6 hours (unlike biliary colic <6 hours). CHARACTER: SHARP or ACHING, CONSTANT (NOT colicky). AGGRAVATING: WORSE WITH DEEP BREATHING, movement, and fatty food intake. Trigger is fatty meals. RELIEVING: NOTHING relieves pain effectively. Position changes don't help. Pain persists despite rest. TIMING: CONSTANT (not episodic like biliary colic). Lasts >6 hours continuously. SEVERITY: 7-10/10, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: NAUSEA AND VOMITING very common, fever (100.5-102°F) within 12-24 hours, MURPHY'S SIGN (inspiratory arrest during right upper quadrant (RUQ) palpation), anorexia. May have jaundice if common bile duct involved. KEY POSITIVES: right upper quadrant (RUQ) tenderness, Murphy's sign, post-fatty meal onset, fever. KEY NEGATIVES: Diarrhea uncommon. Left-sided pain rules out. Pain resolving <1-2 hours suggests biliary colic not cholecystitis.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, acute, hours
  Negative indicators: chronic, months, gradual

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: right upper quadrant, RUQ, right subcostal, epigastric
  Negative indicators: left side, lower abdomen, LLQ
  Anatomical type: right_upper

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: constant, hours, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, minutes, episodic

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, stabbing, severe, colicky
  Negative indicators: burning, crampy, dull

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: fatty foods, deep inspiration, movement
  Negative indicators: fasting, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: avoiding fatty foods, pain medication
  Negative indicators: eating, movement

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant, after meals, progressive
  Negative indicators: intermittent, unrelated to food

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe, 7, 8, 9, 10
  Negative indicators: mild, 1, 2, 3

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 High fever >102.5°F with rigors - suggests gangrenous cholecystitis or ascending cholangitis

  🚨 Jaundice with confusion or sepsis - ascending cholangitis, needs urgent ERCP

  🚨 Severe right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain with hypotension - gallbladder perforation or septic shock, call 911

  🚨 Elderly or diabetic patients - higher risk of gangrenous cholecystitis and perforation
