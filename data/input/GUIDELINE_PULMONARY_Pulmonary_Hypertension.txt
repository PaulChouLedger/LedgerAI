CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • pulmonary hypertension
  • high blood pressure in lungs
  • shortness of breath
  • chest pain
  • fatigue

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual over months to years. LOCATION: Diffuse chest. DURATION: Chronic, progressive. CHARACTER: Progressive dyspnea, chest pain. AGGRAVATING: Exertion, lying flat. RELIEVING: Oxygen, vasodilators. TIMING: Chronic, progressive. SEVERITY: Moderate to severe (5-8/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Progressive dyspnea, chest pain, fatigue, syncope, leg swelling. KEY POSITIVES: Progressive dyspnea, chest pain, fatigue. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no wheezing (unless reactive airway disease).

N/A

Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual over months to years, progressive, slow onset
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, immediate

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: diffuse chest
  Negative indicators: localized, one side only, specific point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: chronic, progressive, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: progressive dyspnea, chest pain
  Negative indicators: no fever, no wheezing, mild

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: exertion, lying flat, activity
  Negative indicators: oxygen, vasodilators, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: oxygen, vasodilators, rest
  Negative indicators: exertion, lying flat, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: chronic, progressive, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate to severe, 5-8/10, 5, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or respiratory distress

  🚨 Cyanosis or oxygen saturation <90%

  🚨 Syncope or near-syncope

  🚨 Signs of right heart failure

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure

  🚨 Severe chest pain

  🚨 Signs of shock
