CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • shortness of breath
  • chest pressure
  • difficulty breathing
  • lightheadedness

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Can be acute (trauma, rupture) or subacute (effusion accumulation). LOCATION: Chest (retrosternal pressure), generalized dyspnea. DURATION: Progressive over hours to days. CHARACTER: Dyspnea, chest pressure, feeling of impending doom. AGGRAVATING: Lying flat. RELIEVING: Sitting up, leaning forward. TIMING: Progressive worsening. SEVERITY: Severe respiratory distress. KEY POSITIVES: Beck's triad (hypotension, muffled heart sounds, JVD), pulsus paradoxus, tachycardia, dyspnea, recent pericarditis or trauma or malignancy. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT pleuritic pain, NOT unilateral symptoms.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: acute, trauma, rupture, subacute, effusion accumulation
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: chest, retrosternal pressure, generalized dyspnea
  Negative indicators: pleuritic pain, unilateral symptoms, localized

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: progressive, over hours to days, worsening
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: dyspnea, chest pressure, feeling of impending doom
  Negative indicators: pleuritic pain, sharp, stabbing, no symptoms

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: lying flat
  Negative indicators: sitting up, leaning forward, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: sitting up, leaning forward
  Negative indicators: lying flat, activity, exertion

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: progressive worsening, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe respiratory distress, severe
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, no symptoms

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Hypotension or shock

  🚨 Muffled or distant heart sounds

  🚨 Jugular venous distension (JVD)

  🚨 Pulsus paradoxus

  🚨 Tachycardia

  🚨 Recent chest trauma

  🚨 Recent cardiac procedure or catheterization

  🚨 History of pericarditis or malignancy

  🚨 Altered mental status
