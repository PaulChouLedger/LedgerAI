Condition: Testicular Torsion
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: emergent
Prevalence: rare
ICD-10 Code: N44.00
SNOMED Code: 59952004
Sex: male

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • testicular pain
  • testicle pain
  • scrotal pain
  • groin pain
  • lower abdominal pain
  • severe testicle pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN, ACUTE onset within minutes to hours. Bimodal distribution: neonates and adolescents (12-18 years peak). May occur after trauma or during sleep. LOCATION: Affected testicle, may radiate to lower abdomen or groin. DURATION: CONSTANT pain since onset, progressively worsening. CHARACTER: SEVERE, SHARP, CONSTANT pain. AGGRAVATING: Movement, palpation of testicle. RELIEVING: NOTHING relieves pain. Lying still may decrease intensity slightly. TIMING: CONSTANT and unrelenting, worsens over time. Salvage rate <10% after 24 hours. SEVERITY: 9-10/10, SEVERE (patient often writhing in pain). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Nausea and vomiting (50%), high-riding testicle, horizontal lie of testicle, absent cremasteric reflex, scrotal swelling and erythema. NO relief with testicular elevation (negative Prehn's sign). KEY POSITIVES: Sudden severe testicular pain, adolescent male, nausea, high-riding testicle, absent cremasteric reflex. KEY NEGATIVES: Gradual onset over days suggests epididymitis. Relief with elevation suggests epididymitis.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Any testicular pain >6 hours - irreversible ischemia developing, IMMEDIATE ER

  🚨 Sudden severe testicular pain - ALWAYS emergent until proven otherwise

  🚨 Absent cremasteric reflex with testicular pain - high suspicion for torsion

  🚨 Nausea/vomiting with testicular pain - torsion until proven otherwise
