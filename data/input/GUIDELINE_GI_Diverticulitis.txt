CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • left lower abdominal pain
  • left lower quadrant (LLQ) pain
  • lower left side pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL to SUDDEN onset within 1-3 days (subacute). Not as dramatic as appendicitis. LOCATION: LEFT LOWER QUADRANT (LLQ) - key differentiator from appendicitis (right lower quadrant (RLQ). LOCALIZED and CONSTANT. Sometimes palpable tender mass. DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting hours to days (not episodic). Persists continuously. CHARACTER: ACHING or SHARP, CONSTANT pain. Steady, unrelenting quality. AGGRAVATING: MOVEMENT worsens pain. Bowel movements may increase discomfort. RELIEVING: Rest and lying still provide slight relief. NOTHING fully relieves. Antibiotics needed for resolution. TIMING: CONSTANT throughout. May worsen progressively. Not wave-like or crampy. SEVERITY: 6-9/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: LOW-GRADE FEVER (100-101°F), change in bowel habits (DIARRHEA or constipation), nausea, anorexia. KEY POSITIVES: left lower quadrant (LLQ) tenderness, fever, age >40, known diverticulosis, low-fiber diet history. KEY NEGATIVES: RIGHT-sided pain suggests appendicitis. HIGH FEVER >102°F suggests abscess/perforation. PROFUSE diarrhea suggests gastroenteritis. Age <40 makes unlikely.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, subacute, 1-3 days
  Negative indicators: sudden, chronic, months

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: left lower quadrant, LLQ, left lower, localized
  Negative indicators: right side, RLQ, upper abdomen, diffuse
  Anatomical type: left_only

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: constant, hours, days, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, minutes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: aching, sharp, constant, steady
  Negative indicators: crampy, burning, pressure-like

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: movement, bowel movements
  Negative indicators: eating, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, lying still
  Negative indicators: movement, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant, progressive
  Negative indicators: wave-like, crampy, intermittent

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate, severe, 6, 7, 8, 9, 6-9/10
  Negative indicators: mild, 1, 2, 3

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe left lower quadrant (LLQ) pain with high fever >102°F and rigors - diverticular abscess, needs computed tomography (CT) and possible drainage

  🚨 Sudden severe pain with peritoneal signs - perforation with peritonitis, call 911

  🚨 Unable to tolerate oral intake with vomiting - possible obstruction from inflammation

  🚨 Immunocompromised patients - higher risk of perforation and sepsis
