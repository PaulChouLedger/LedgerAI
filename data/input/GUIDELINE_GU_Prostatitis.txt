CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • pelvic pain
  • perineal pain
  • painful urination
  • dysuria
  • lower abdominal pain
  • rectal pain
  • difficulty urinating

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: ACUTE onset over hours to days. More common in men 30-50 years. LOCATION: Perineum (between scrotum and rectum), suprapubic, lower back, or rectal area. Diffuse pelvic discomfort. DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting days. CHARACTER: DULL, ACHING, CONSTANT deep pelvic/perineal pain. May be SHARP with urination. AGGRAVATING: Urination, bowel movements, prolonged sitting worsen pain. RELIEVING: Urination may briefly relieve pressure but causes dysuria. Lying down may help slightly. TIMING: CONSTANT baseline pain with exacerbations during urination/defecation. SEVERITY: 6-8/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: High fever with rigors, dysuria (painful urination), urinary frequency and urgency, hesitancy, weak stream, perineal fullness. Tender, boggy prostate on exam (if performed). May have gross hematuria. KEY POSITIVES: Fever + dysuria + perineal pain, urinary symptoms, recent urinary tract infection (UTI) or instrumentation. KEY NEGATIVES: Absence of fever suggests chronic prostatitis or prostatodynia.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 High fever >103°F with rigors - urosepsis risk, may need admission

  🚨 Complete urinary retention - bladder outlet obstruction, needs catheter

  🚨 Hypotension with fever - septic shock, call 911

  🚨 Severe prostatic tenderness - prostatic abscess possible
