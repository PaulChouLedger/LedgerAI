CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • right upper abdominal pain
  • right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain
  • pain after eating

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset 30 minutes to 2 hours AFTER EATING fatty or greasy meals. RECURRENT pattern - patient usually has had similar prior episodes. LOCATION: Right upper quadrant (RUQ) or epigastric. May RADIATE TO RIGHT SHOULDER OR BACK. DURATION: Each episode lasts 30 minutes to 6 hours (shorter than cholecystitis >6 hours), then COMPLETELY RESOLVES. EPISODIC (not constant). CHARACTER: STEADY, SEVERE pain (NOT crampy). Described as intense pressure or aching. AGGRAVATING: Triggered by FATTY OR GREASY MEALS (fried foods, fatty meats, dairy). Eating worsens or precipitates attacks. RELIEVING: Pain SPONTANEOUSLY RESOLVES after 30min-6 hours. Patient is PAIN-FREE between episodes. Rest may help during episode. TIMING: EPISODIC attacks with complete resolution between. Comes and goes. Pattern repeats over weeks to months. SEVERITY: 7-10/10 during episodes, 0/10 between episodes. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Nausea (minimal or no vomiting), bloating, belching. NO FEVER (fever indicates cholecystitis). KEY POSITIVES: Food triggers, episodic pattern, pain-free intervals, right upper quadrant (RUQ) location. KEY NEGATIVES: CONSTANT pain suggests cholecystitis not colic. Fever rules out simple colic. Jaundice suggests bile duct stone.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, 30 minutes after eating, 2 hours after eating, after fatty meals, after greasy meals, recurrent
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, constant

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: right upper quadrant, RUQ, epigastric, radiates to right shoulder, radiates to back
  Negative indicators: left side, lower abdomen, diffuse

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: 30 minutes, 6 hours, episodic, completely resolves
  Negative indicators: constant, persistent, unrelenting

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: steady, severe, intense pressure, aching, not crampy
  Negative indicators: crampy, intermittent, mild

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: fatty meals, greasy meals, fried foods, fatty meats, dairy, eating
  Negative indicators: rest, fasting, water

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: spontaneously resolves, pain-free between episodes, rest
  Negative indicators: medication, constant pain

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: episodic, comes and goes, complete resolution between, recurrent pattern
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 10, 7-10/10, 7, severe during episodes, 0 between episodes
  Negative indicators: mild, constant severe, 1-5/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Pain lasting >6 hours with fever - suggests acute cholecystitis, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Jaundice (yellow skin/eyes) - bile duct obstruction, needs urgent imaging and possible ERCP

  🚨 Severe pain with vomiting and unable to tolerate liquids - dehydration risk
