CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • drug rash
  • medication reaction
  • allergic drug reaction
  • skin reaction to medicine
  • drug allergy
  • medication allergy
  • drug hypersensitivity

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within hours to days after starting new medication. LOCATION: GENERALIZED or localized to specific areas. DURATION: Develops over hours to days, may persist for days to weeks. CHARACTER: ERYTHEMATOUS (red), PRURITIC (itchy), may be MACULOPAPULAR (flat and raised) or VESICULAR (blistering). AGGRAVATING: SCRATCHING, heat, stress. RELIEVING: DISCONTINUING offending drug, antihistamines, topical steroids. TIMING: Develops within hours to days of drug exposure. SEVERITY: 4-8/10 itching, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SEVERE ITCHING (pruritus), erythema, may have fever, may have systemic symptoms (nausea, vomiting, diarrhea). KEY POSITIVES: Erythema, pruritus, recent medication history, positive drug challenge. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever >102°F (rules out serious drug reaction), no systemic symptoms (unless severe).

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rash with fever >102°F - possible serious drug reaction, call 911

  🚨 Rash with severe systemic symptoms - possible anaphylaxis, call 911

  🚨 Rash involving mucous membranes - eyes, mouth, genitals - Stevens-Johnson syndrome, call 911

  🚨 Rash with difficulty breathing - possible anaphylaxis, call 911

  🚨 Rash with signs of secondary infection - fever, pus, increasing pain - needs urgent evaluation
