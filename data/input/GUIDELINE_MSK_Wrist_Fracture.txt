CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • wrist pain
  • wrist injury
  • broken wrist
  • wrist swelling
  • can't move wrist
  • wrist deformity
  • fell on wrist

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset during trauma - fall on outstretched hand (FOOSH), direct blow, or crush injury. LOCATION: Distal radius (most common), scaphoid, or other carpal bones. DURATION: Immediate severe pain that persists. CHARACTER: SEVERE, SHARP, CONSTANT pain. AGGRAVATING: ANY wrist movement, gripping, weight bearing. RELIEVING: IMMOBILIZATION, ice, elevation, NSAIDs. TIMING: Immediate severe pain that persists until immobilization. SEVERITY: 7-10/10, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SWELLING within hours, BRUISING develops over 24-48 hours, DEFORMITY (dinner fork deformity in Colles fracture), inability to move wrist, numbness/tingling in fingers (median nerve compression). May have audible 'snap' or 'crack' at injury. KEY POSITIVES: Bony tenderness over fracture site, deformity, swelling, limited range of motion, positive compression test. KEY NEGATIVES: No open wound (rules out open fracture), no crepitus unless severe.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, during trauma, fall on outstretched hand, FOOSH, direct blow, crush injury
  Negative indicators: gradual, progressive, slow onset, days to weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: distal radius, scaphoid, carpal bones, wrist, wrist joint
  Negative indicators: elbow, shoulder, hand, forearm

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: immediate, persists, constant, ongoing
  Negative indicators: brief, minutes, hours, transient

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe, sharp, constant, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: mild, dull, intermittent, aching

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: wrist movement, gripping, weight bearing, any movement
  Negative indicators: immobilization, ice, elevation, NSAIDs

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: immobilization, ice, elevation, NSAIDs, rest
  Negative indicators: wrist movement, gripping, weight bearing, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: immediate, persists until immobilization, constant, ongoing
  Negative indicators: delayed, intermittent, variable, episodic

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe, 7-10/10, 7, 8, 9, 10, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, moderate, 4-6/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Open wound with bone visible - open fracture, call 911

  🚨 Severe deformity with neurovascular compromise - cold, pale, pulseless hand, call 911

  🚨 Severe pain with numbness/tingling - possible compartment syndrome or nerve injury

  🚨 Elderly patients with minimal trauma - possible pathological fracture, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 High-energy trauma - possible associated injuries, needs urgent evaluation
