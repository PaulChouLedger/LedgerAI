CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • flank pain
  • back pain
  • painful urination
  • burning with urination

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN to GRADUAL onset within 1-3 days. Acute presentation. LOCATION: FLANK PAIN (unilateral or bilateral) with cerebrovascular accident (CVA) (costovertebral angle) tenderness. Lower abdominal discomfort. DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting hours to days. Dysuria persists with each urination. CHARACTER: ACHING, DULL flank pain. Burning sensation with urination (dysuria). AGGRAVATING: URINATION worsens dysuria (burning pain). Movement may worsen flank pain. Dehydration worsens. RELIEVING: Antibiotics provide relief within 24-48 hours. Hydration helps. Urinary analgesics reduce dysuria. Position changes don't significantly help. TIMING: CONSTANT flank ache. Dysuria with EACH void. Symptoms persist until treated. SEVERITY: 6-8/10 flank pain, moderate to severe. Dysuria can be very uncomfortable. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: HIGH FEVER (>101°F) with RIGORS (shaking chills), DYSURIA (burning), frequency, urgency, hematuria, nausea, vomiting, malaise. KEY POSITIVES: Classic triad: fever + flank pain + dysuria. cerebrovascular accident (CVA) tenderness. More common in FEMALES. KEY NEGATIVES: NO FEVER with dysuria suggests cystitis not pyelonephritis. Flank pain WITHOUT urinary symptoms suggests kidney stone.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sepsis (hypotension, altered mental status, high fever) - urosepsis, call 911

  🚨 Pregnant patient with pyelonephritis - high risk, needs hospitalization

  🚨 Diabetic with pyelonephritis - higher risk of complications

  🚨 Severe nausea/vomiting with inability to tolerate oral antibiotics - needs IV antibiotics
