DIAGNOSTIC GUIDELINE: CARDIOGENIC SHOCK
================================================================================

Condition: Cardiogenic Shock
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: emergent
Prevalence: rare
ICD-10 Code: R57.0
SNOMED Code: 27942005
Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • severe shortness of breath
  • chest pain
  • can't breathe
  • confusion
  • cold extremities

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Acute, often following myocardial infarction (MI) or acute heart failure. LOCATION: Generalized, systemic hypoperfusion. DURATION: Continuous, life-threatening. CHARACTER: Severe dyspnea, chest pain (if myocardial infarction ), altered mental status, cold/clammy skin. AGGRAVATING: Reduced cardiac output. RELIEVING: Inotropic support, revascularization. TIMING: Acute decompensation. SEVERITY: Critical, life-threatening. KEY POSITIVES: Hypotension (systolic blood pressure (SBP) <90 mmHg), tachycardia, cold clammy extremities, altered mental status, oliguria (low urine output), pulmonary edema, recent myocardial infarction (MI), severe heart failure. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT septic shock (warm extremities), NOT hypovolemic shock (responds to fluids).

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: acute, following myocardial infarction, acute heart failure
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: generalized, systemic hypoperfusion
  Negative indicators: localized, one area only, chest

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: continuous, life-threatening, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe dyspnea, chest pain, altered mental status, cold clammy skin
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, warm extremities, no symptoms

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: reduced cardiac output
  Negative indicators: inotropic support, revascularization, medication

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: inotropic support, revascularization
  Negative indicators: reduced cardiac output, activity, exertion

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: acute decompensation, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: critical, life-threatening, severe
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, no symptoms

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Hypotension <90 mmHg systolic

  🚨 Altered mental status or confusion

  🚨 Cold, clammy skin

  🚨 Oliguria or anuria

  🚨 Pulmonary edema

  🚨 Recent myocardial infarction (MI) or severe heart failure

  🚨 Lactate elevation

  🚨 Multi-organ failure
