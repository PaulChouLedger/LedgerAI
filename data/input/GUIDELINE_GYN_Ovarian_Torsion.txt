Condition: Ovarian Torsion

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • pelvic pain
  • lower abdominal pain
  • severe pelvic pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within minutes to hours. Acute, dramatic presentation. Often associated with physical activity or position change. LOCATION: UNILATERAL LOWER ABDOMINAL/PELVIC PAIN. Adnexal region, one side (more common on RIGHT). Ipsilateral lower quadrant. DURATION: CONSTANT from onset. Pain persists and worsens without intervention. Lasts hours to days if not surgically addressed. CHARACTER: SUDDEN, SEVERE, SHARP pain often described as 'WORST PAIN EVER'. Stabbing or twisting sensation. Excruciating. AGGRAVATING: MOVEMENT worsens pain. Physical activity may precipitate torsion. Position changes intensify discomfort. Jarring unbearable. RELIEVING: NOTHING helps. Position doesn't relieve. Analgesics provide minimal relief. Requires surgical detorsion for relief. TIMING: CONSTANT and unrelenting from onset. Progressively worsens as ovarian ischemia progresses. Not episodic. SEVERITY: 8-10/10, very severe and debilitating. Patient in extreme distress. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: NAUSEA AND VOMITING (70%), sometimes low-grade fever. Appears in severe distress. KEY POSITIVES: Severe unilateral tenderness, adnexal mass on exam, known cyst, pregnancy, nausea out of proportion. SURGICAL EMERGENCY. KEY NEGATIVES: Bilateral pain less likely. Gradual onset suggests alternative.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 SURGICAL EMERGENCY - delayed treatment leads to ovarian necrosis and loss of ovary

  🚨 Sudden severe unilateral pelvic pain in woman - needs urgent ultrasound with Doppler

  🚨 Pregnancy with ovarian torsion - corpus luteum cyst torsion, needs urgent surgery

  🚨 ANY delay in diagnosis risks permanent ovarian damage
