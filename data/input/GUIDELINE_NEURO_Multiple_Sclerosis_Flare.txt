CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • multiple sclerosis
  • MS flare
  • neurological symptoms
  • muscle weakness
  • numbness
  • vision problems
  • balance problems

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over days to weeks. LOCATION: VARIABLE - may affect any part of nervous system. DURATION: Develops over days to weeks, may persist for weeks to months. CHARACTER: VARIABLE symptoms depending on affected area. AGGRAVATING: HEAT, stress, fatigue. RELIEVING: STEROIDS, disease-modifying therapies. TIMING: Gradual onset, develops over days to weeks. SEVERITY: 4-8/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: VARIABLE - muscle weakness, numbness, tingling, vision problems, balance problems, may have cognitive changes. KEY POSITIVES: Variable symptoms, may have previous MS diagnosis, may have MRI changes. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe neurological deficits with altered mental status - possible severe MS flare, call 911

  🚨 Signs of increased intracranial pressure - severe headache, vomiting, altered mental status - call 911

  🚨 Severe hypertension with neurological deficits - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of brainstem involvement - dysphagia, dysarthria, ataxia - call 911

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure - difficulty breathing, cyanosis, altered mental status - call 911
