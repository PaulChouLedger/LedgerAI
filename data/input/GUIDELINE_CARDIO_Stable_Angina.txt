CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain with exertion
  • chest pressure with activity
  • exertional chest pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual with exertion, predictable pattern. LOCATION: RETROSTERNAL or left chest. RADIATES to left arm, jaw, shoulder. DURATION: Brief, typically 2-10 minutes. CHARACTER: Pressure, tightness, heaviness. AGGRAVATING: EXERTION, cold weather, heavy meals, emotional stress. RELIEVING: REST (within minutes) or nitroglycerin. TIMING: Reproducible with similar exertion levels. SEVERITY: 4-6/10, consistent. KEY POSITIVES: Predictable with exertion, relieved by rest in <5 minutes, relieved by nitroglycerin, history of CAD or risk factors. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT at rest (unstable angina), NOT unpredictable.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, with exertion, predictable pattern
  Negative indicators: sudden, at rest, unpredictable

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: retrosternal, left chest, radiates to left arm, radiates to jaw, radiates to shoulder
  Negative indicators: right side only, no radiation, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: brief, 2-10 minutes, short duration
  Negative indicators: prolonged, >10 minutes, continuous

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: pressure, tightness, heaviness
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, burning, crushing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: exertion, cold weather, heavy meals, emotional stress, activity
  Negative indicators: rest, nitroglycerin, medication

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, within minutes, nitroglycerin
  Negative indicators: exertion, activity, cold weather

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: reproducible, similar exertion levels, predictable
  Negative indicators: unpredictable, at rest, random

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 4-6/10, consistent, 4, 5, 6
  Negative indicators: severe, 7-10/10, mild, 1-3/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Changing pattern (unstable angina)

  🚨 Occurs at rest

  🚨 Increasing frequency or severity

  🚨 Prolonged duration >10 minutes

  🚨 Poor response to nitroglycerin

  🚨 New onset in past 2 weeks
