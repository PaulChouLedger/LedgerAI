CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • knee pain
  • knee injury
  • twisted knee
  • knee swelling
  • knee locked
  • knee giving out
  • can't straighten knee

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset during activity - twisting, pivoting, direct blow, or hyperextension. Common in sports, falls, or accidents. LOCATION: Anterior knee (patellar), medial knee (MCL), lateral knee (LCL), or posterior knee depending on mechanism. DURATION: Immediate pain that may worsen over first 24-48 hours. CHARACTER: SHARP initial pain, then THROBBING and ACHING. May have 'popping' or 'snapping' sound. AGGRAVATING: WEIGHT BEARING, walking, stairs, squatting, knee flexion/extension. RELIEVING: REST, ICE, elevation, NSAIDs. TIMING: Immediate pain, swelling develops over hours, stiffness increases over 24-48 hours. SEVERITY: 5-9/10, severe with activity. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SWELLING within hours (effusion), BRUISING develops over 24-48 hours, STIFFNESS, difficulty walking or bearing weight, knee may 'give way' or 'lock'. May have audible 'pop' at injury. KEY POSITIVES: Joint effusion, tenderness over affected ligaments, positive Lachman test (ACL), positive valgus/varus stress tests (MCL/LCL), positive McMurray test (meniscus). KEY NEGATIVES: No bony tenderness over patella or tibial plateau (rules out fracture), no obvious deformity.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, during activity, twisting, pivoting, direct blow, hyperextension
  Negative indicators: gradual, progressive, slow onset, days to weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: anterior knee, patellar, medial knee, MCL, lateral knee, LCL, posterior knee
  Negative indicators: hip, ankle, thigh, calf

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: immediate, 24-48 hours, hours, persistent
  Negative indicators: minutes, seconds, chronic, weeks to months

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, throbbing, aching, popping, snapping
  Negative indicators: dull, burning, numbness, tingling

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: weight bearing, walking, stairs, squatting, knee flexion, knee extension
  Negative indicators: rest, ice, elevation, NSAIDs

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, ice, elevation, NSAIDs, immobilization
  Negative indicators: weight bearing, walking, stairs, squatting

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: immediate, hours, 24-48 hours, progressive
  Negative indicators: delayed, days later, chronic, intermittent

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe, 5-9/10, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, moderate, 4/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Inability to bear weight immediately - possible fracture or severe ligament injury

  🚨 Severe deformity or obvious fracture - obvious knee fracture, call 911

  🚨 Knee locked in fixed position - possible meniscal tear or loose body, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Severe swelling with severe pain - possible hemarthrosis or severe ligament injury

  🚨 Numbness or tingling below knee - possible nerve injury or compartment syndrome
