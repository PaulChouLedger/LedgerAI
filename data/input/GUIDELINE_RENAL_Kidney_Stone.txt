CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • kidney stone
  • renal stone
  • nephrolithiasis
  • flank pain
  • back pain
  • urinary pain
  • stone pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within minutes to hours. LOCATION: UNILATERAL flank pain, may radiate to groin. DURATION: Lasts hours to days. CHARACTER: SEVERE, SHARP, COLIC pain. AGGRAVATING: MOVEMENT, urination. RELIEVING: PAIN medications, hydration. TIMING: Sudden onset, lasts hours to days. SEVERITY: 8-10/10, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SEVERE FLANK PAIN, may have nausea, vomiting, hematuria, may have fever if infected. KEY POSITIVES: Severe flank pain, hematuria, positive imaging. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

N/A

  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic

  Positive indicators: flank, unilateral

DURATION:

CHARACTER:

  Positive indicators: movement, urination, medications

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: pain medications

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: unrelenting

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 8-10/10, severe

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe pain with fever - possible infected stone, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe infection - fever, chills, rigors - call 911

  🚨 Severe hypertension with neurological deficits - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe metabolic acidosis - deep breathing, confusion, coma - call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe electrolyte imbalances - confusion, seizures, cardiac arrhythmias - call 911
