CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • pyelonephritis
  • kidney infection
  • renal infection
  • upper UTI
  • flank pain
  • back pain
  • fever with urinary symptoms

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within hours to days. LOCATION: UNILATERAL or bilateral kidney involvement. DURATION: Develops over hours to days, may persist for days to weeks. CHARACTER: SEVERE flank pain, fever, chills. AGGRAVATING: MOVEMENT, urination. RELIEVING: ANTIBIOTICS, pain medications. TIMING: Sudden onset, develops over hours to days. SEVERITY: 7-9/10, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SEVERE FLANK PAIN, fever, chills, nausea, vomiting, may have hematuria. KEY POSITIVES: Severe flank pain, fever, chills, positive urinalysis, positive blood cultures. KEY NEGATIVES: No neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 High fever with rigors - possible sepsis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe infection - fever, chills, rigors - call 911

  🚨 Severe hypertension with neurological deficits - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe metabolic acidosis - deep breathing, confusion, coma - call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe electrolyte imbalances - confusion, seizures, cardiac arrhythmias - call 911
