CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • skin infection
  • impetigo
  • honey crusted rash
  • skin sores
  • contagious rash
  • skin blisters
  • school sores

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over 2-3 days, often following minor skin trauma or insect bite. LOCATION: FACE (around nose, mouth), hands, or anywhere on body. DURATION: Develops over 2-3 days, may persist for 1-2 weeks without treatment. CHARACTER: VESICULAR (blistering) lesions that rupture and form HONEY-COLORED CRUSTS. AGGRAVATING: SCRATCHING, heat, sweating. RELIEVING: ANTIBIOTICS, keeping lesions clean and dry. TIMING: Develops over 2-3 days, peaks at 3-5 days. SEVERITY: 2-4/10 pain, mild to moderate. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: MILD ITCHING (pruritus), honey-colored crusts, may have regional lymphadenopathy (swollen lymph nodes), may have fever (low-grade). KEY POSITIVES: Vesicular lesions, honey-colored crusts, positive bacterial culture. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever >102°F, no systemic symptoms, no deep tissue involvement.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rash with fever >102°F - possible systemic infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with severe systemic symptoms - possible sepsis, call 911

  🚨 Rash with signs of deep tissue involvement - cellulitis, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with signs of secondary infection - fever, pus, increasing pain - needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash involving >30% of body surface - severe infection, needs urgent evaluation
