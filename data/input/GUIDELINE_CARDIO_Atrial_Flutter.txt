CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • palpitations
  • irregular heartbeat
  • fast heartbeat
  • fluttering in chest

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Can be sudden or gradual. LOCATION: Chest (palpitations). DURATION: Minutes to persistent. CHARACTER: Regular RAPID palpitations, "flutter" sensation. AGGRAVATING: Exertion, stress. RELIEVING: Cardioversion, rate control medications. TIMING: Paroxysmal or persistent. SEVERITY: Discomfort, not severe pain. KEY POSITIVES: Regular rapid heart rate (often 150 bpm with 2:1 block), dyspnea, lightheadedness, sawtooth pattern on electrocardiogram (ECG), history of AFib or structural heart disease. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT irregularly irregular (AFib), NOT sudden start/stop (supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) typically).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: can be sudden, gradual, variable
  Negative indicators: chronic, constant, unrelenting

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: chest, palpitations
  Negative indicators: localized, one area only, extremities

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: minutes to persistent, variable
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, hours only

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: regular rapid palpitations, flutter sensation
  Negative indicators: irregularly irregular, sudden start stop, slow

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: exertion, stress
  Negative indicators: cardioversion, rate control medications, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: cardioversion, rate control medications
  Negative indicators: exertion, stress, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: paroxysmal, persistent
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: discomfort, not severe pain, moderate
  Negative indicators: severe, painful, mild
