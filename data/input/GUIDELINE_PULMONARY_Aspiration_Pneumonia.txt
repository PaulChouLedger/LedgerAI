CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • aspiration
  • choking
  • vomiting and breathing problems
  • food in lungs
  • aspiration pneumonia

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden during or shortly after aspiration event. LOCATION: Dependent lung regions (right lower lobe most common). DURATION: Acute onset, can persist for days to weeks. CHARACTER: Dyspnea, cough, chest discomfort. AGGRAVATING: Lying flat, continued aspiration risk. RELIEVING: Upright positioning, suctioning, antibiotics. TIMING: Acute, often during eating or vomiting. SEVERITY: Moderate to severe (6-8/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Fever, productive cough (may be foul-smelling), dyspnea, chest pain, altered mental status (if hypoxic). KEY POSITIVES: Recent aspiration event, fever, productive cough, dyspnea. KEY NEGATIVES: No chest pain (unless severe), no wheezing (unless reactive airway disease).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden during or shortly after aspiration event, acute, immediate
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: dependent lung regions, right lower lobe most common, lung
  Negative indicators: unilateral, one side only, upper lobes

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: acute onset, can persist for days to weeks, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: dyspnea, cough, chest discomfort
  Negative indicators: no chest pain, no wheezing, mild

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: lying flat, continued aspiration risk, activity
  Negative indicators: upright positioning, suctioning, antibiotics

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: upright positioning, suctioning, antibiotics
  Negative indicators: lying flat, continued aspiration risk, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: acute, often during eating or vomiting, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate to severe, 6-8/10, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-5/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or respiratory distress

  🚨 High fever >101.5°F

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Cyanosis or oxygen saturation <90%

  🚨 Hemoptysis or blood in sputum

  🚨 Shock or hypotension

  🚨 Inability to protect airway

  🚨 Recurrent aspiration events
