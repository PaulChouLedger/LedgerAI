CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain
  • palpitations
  • chest discomfort
  • irregular heartbeat

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Episodic, often asymptomatic discovered incidentally. LOCATION: Left chest or precordial. DURATION: Brief episodes of palpitations or atypical chest pain. CHARACTER: Sharp, brief, atypical chest pain NOT typical angina. Palpitations. AGGRAVATING: Anxiety, caffeine, dehydration. RELIEVING: Rest, beta-blockers. TIMING: Episodic, unpredictable. SEVERITY: Mild, 2-4/10. KEY POSITIVES: Young female (classic), mid-systolic click on exam, late systolic murmur, palpitations, atypical chest pain, anxiety, thin body habitus. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT exertional angina, NOT dyspnea typically (unless severe regurgitation).

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: episodic, often asymptomatic, discovered incidentally
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, minutes

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: left chest, precordial
  Negative indicators: right side only, no radiation, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: brief episodes, short duration
  Negative indicators: continuous, unrelenting, hours

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, brief, atypical chest pain, not typical angina, palpitations
  Negative indicators: exertional angina, dyspnea, crushing, pressure

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: anxiety, caffeine, dehydration
  Negative indicators: rest, beta-blockers, medication

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, beta-blockers
  Negative indicators: anxiety, caffeine, dehydration

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: episodic, unpredictable
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: mild, 2-4/10, 2, 3, 4
  Negative indicators: moderate, 5-10/10, severe

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe mitral regurgitation

  🚨 Heart failure symptoms

  🚨 Atrial fibrillation

  🚨 Endocarditis

  🚨 Syncope

  🚨 Severe palpitations

  🚨 Family history of sudden cardiac death
