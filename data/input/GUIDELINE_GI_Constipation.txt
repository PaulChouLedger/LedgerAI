CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • stomach pain
  • cramping
  • bloating
  • can't have bowel movement
  • constipation

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL over days to weeks (not acute or sudden). Chronic, progressive pattern. LOCATION: LOWER ABDOMEN, diffuse. Cramping throughout lower belly (not localized). DURATION: Symptoms persist for days to weeks. Cramping episodes last minutes to hours, come and go. CHARACTER: CRAMPY, ACHY abdominal discomfort. NOT sharp or stabbing. Dull, bloating sensation. AGGRAVATING: Eating may worsen bloating. Straining worsens discomfort. RELIEVING: Bowel movement or PASSING GAS provides significant relief. Fiber, hydration, laxatives help. TIMING: INTERMITTENT cramping that comes and goes. Better after defecation, worse when haven't gone for days. SEVERITY: 3-6/10, mild to moderate discomfort (not severe). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: BLOATING, sensation of incomplete evacuation, straining, HARD PELLET-LIKE STOOLS, NO bowel movement for 3-7+ days. Usually NO fever, NO vomiting. KEY POSITIVES: Gradual onset, able to pass gas (unlike obstruction), pain relief after defecation, chronic pattern, poor fiber/water intake. KEY NEGATIVES: SUDDEN onset with inability to pass gas suggests obstruction. Vomiting uncommon. SEVERE pain suggests alternative.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, days to weeks, chronic, progressive pattern
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, hours, minutes

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: lower abdomen, diffuse, throughout lower belly, not localized
  Negative indicators: upper abdomen, localized, one quadrant, specific point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: days to weeks, minutes to hours, come and go, persist
  Negative indicators: constant, continuous, unrelenting

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: crampy, achy, discomfort, dull, bloating sensation
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, severe, excruciating

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: eating, straining, bloating
  Negative indicators: bowel movement, passing gas, fiber, hydration

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: bowel movement, passing gas, fiber, hydration, laxatives, defecation
  Negative indicators: eating, straining

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: intermittent, comes and goes, better after defecation, worse when haven't gone
  Negative indicators: constant, continuous, unrelenting

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 3-6/10, mild, moderate, 3, 4, 5, 6, discomfort
  Negative indicators: severe, 7-10/10, excruciating

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sudden onset severe constipation with vomiting - possible bowel obstruction, needs urgent imaging

  🚨 Complete inability to pass gas - suggests obstruction not simple constipation

  🚨 Blood in stool with weight loss - concern for colon cancer, needs colonoscopy

  🚨 New onset constipation in elderly - higher risk of serious pathology
