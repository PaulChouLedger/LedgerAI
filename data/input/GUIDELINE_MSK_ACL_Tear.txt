CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • knee pain
  • ACL tear
  • knee injury
  • knee giving out
  • knee instability
  • knee swelling
  • knee locked

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset during activity - pivoting, cutting, landing, or direct blow. Common in sports requiring sudden direction changes. LOCATION: Anterior knee, may radiate to posterior knee. DURATION: Immediate severe pain that may worsen over first 24-48 hours. CHARACTER: SHARP initial pain, then THROBBING and ACHING. AGGRAVATING: WEIGHT BEARING, walking, stairs, pivoting, cutting movements. RELIEVING: REST, ICE, elevation, NSAIDs. TIMING: Immediate pain, swelling develops over hours, stiffness increases over 24-48 hours. SEVERITY: 6-9/10, severe with activity. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SWELLING within hours (effusion), BRUISING develops over 24-48 hours, INSTABILITY (knee 'giving way'), difficulty walking or bearing weight, may have audible 'pop' at injury. KEY POSITIVES: Positive Lachman test, positive anterior drawer test, positive pivot shift test, joint effusion, positive McMurray test (if meniscus involved). KEY NEGATIVES: No bony tenderness over patella or tibial plateau (rules out fracture), no obvious deformity.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, during activity, pivoting, cutting, landing, direct blow
  Negative indicators: gradual, progressive, slow onset, days to weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: anterior knee, posterior knee, knee joint, front of knee
  Negative indicators: hip, ankle, thigh, calf

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: immediate, 24-48 hours, hours, persistent
  Negative indicators: minutes, seconds, chronic, weeks to months

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, throbbing, aching, severe
  Negative indicators: dull, mild, burning, numbness

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: weight bearing, walking, stairs, pivoting, cutting movements
  Negative indicators: rest, ice, elevation, NSAIDs

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, ice, elevation, NSAIDs, immobilization
  Negative indicators: weight bearing, walking, stairs, pivoting

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: immediate, hours, 24-48 hours, progressive
  Negative indicators: delayed, days later, chronic, intermittent

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe, 6-9/10, 6, 7, 8, 9
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, moderate, 4-5/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe pain with inability to bear weight - possible associated fracture or severe injury

  🚨 Severe swelling with severe pain - possible hemarthrosis or severe ligament injury

  🚨 Severe pain with neurological deficits - possible nerve injury, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Severe pain with fever - possible infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Severe pain with night pain - possible malignancy, needs urgent evaluation
