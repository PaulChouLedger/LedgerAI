CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • skin rash
  • contact dermatitis
  • allergic skin reaction
  • skin irritation
  • poison ivy
  • skin allergy
  • itchy rash

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset 24-48 hours after exposure to allergen/irritant. LOCATION: AREAS OF CONTACT with offending substance - hands, face, arms, legs. DURATION: Develops over 24-48 hours, may persist for days to weeks. CHARACTER: ERYTHEMATOUS (red), PRURITIC (itchy), may have VESICLES (small blisters). AGGRAVATING: SCRATCHING, heat, sweating. RELIEVING: AVOIDING trigger, cool compresses, topical steroids, antihistamines. TIMING: Develops 24-48 hours after exposure, peaks at 48-72 hours. SEVERITY: 4-7/10 itching, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SEVERE ITCHING (pruritus), erythema, edema, may have vesicles or bullae, may have secondary infection from scratching. KEY POSITIVES: Erythema, pruritus, vesicles, linear pattern (poison ivy), contact history. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no systemic symptoms, no scaling or crusting (unless chronic).

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rash involving >30% of body surface - severe allergic reaction, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with fever and systemic symptoms - possible serious allergic reaction, call 911

  🚨 Rash involving face, eyes, or genitals - severe allergic reaction, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with difficulty breathing - possible anaphylaxis, call 911

  🚨 Rash with signs of secondary infection - fever, pus, increasing pain - needs urgent evaluation
