CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • pelvic pain
  • lower abdominal pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within minutes. Acute, sharp presentation. Often associated with intercourse, exercise, or pelvic exam. Mid-cycle or just before menses timing. LOCATION: UNILATERAL LOWER ABDOMINAL/PELVIC PAIN. Adnexal region, one side. Lower quadrant on affected side. DURATION: Initially severe for hours, then gradually resolves over 24-48 hours. Self-limited course. CHARACTER: Initially SHARP, STABBING pain (7-9/10), then becomes DULL/ACHING as resolves. Sudden sharp onset, gradual dull resolution. AGGRAVATING: Initial rupture moment is most painful. Movement may worsen initially. Activity precipitated rupture. RELIEVING: Pain GRADUALLY IMPROVES OVER 24-48 HOURS spontaneously (key differentiator from torsion which worsens). Rest helps. Analgesics reduce pain. TIMING: SUDDEN onset, then IMPROVES progressively over 24-48 hours. Initial severe sharp pain, then gradual dull ache that fades. NOT constant or worsening. SEVERITY: Initially 7-9/10, decreases to 2-4/10 over 24-48 hours. Progressive improvement. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Mild nausea, vaginal spotting, pressure sensation. Usually BENIGN and SELF-LIMITED. KEY POSITIVES: Sudden onset, unilateral, IMPROVES over 24-48 hours, mid-cycle timing, known cysts, free fluid on ultrasound. KEY NEGATIVES: WORSENING pain suggests torsion. Hypotension suggests hemorrhagic cyst with blood loss.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, acute, immediate, minutes, hours
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: pelvic, lower abdominal, unilateral, adnexal, vaginal

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: constant
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe, sharp, dull, aching, stabbing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: movement, intercourse, dyspareunia

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant, progressively worsening

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Hypotension or syncope - hemorrhagic cyst with significant blood loss, needs urgent surgery

  🚨 Severe pain not improving or worsening - consider ovarian torsion instead

  🚨 Positive pregnancy test - must rule out ectopic pregnancy first

  🚨 Large amount of free fluid on ultrasound - hemorrhagic cyst, may need intervention
