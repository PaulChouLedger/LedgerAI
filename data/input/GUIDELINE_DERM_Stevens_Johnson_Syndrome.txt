CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • severe skin rash
  • stevens johnson syndrome
  • toxic epidermal necrolysis
  • skin peeling
  • blistering rash
  • mucous membrane rash
  • drug reaction

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within hours to days after drug exposure or infection. LOCATION: GENERALIZED with mucous membrane involvement (eyes, mouth, genitals). DURATION: Rapid progression over hours to days. CHARACTER: ERYTHEMATOUS (red) patches that progress to BULLAE (large blisters) and EPIDERMAL NECROSIS (skin death). AGGRAVATING: TOUCH, heat, movement. RELIEVING: IMMEDIATE discontinuation of offending drug, supportive care, burn unit care. TIMING: Rapid progression over hours to days. SEVERITY: 8-10/10 pain, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: FEVER (100-104°F), malaise, mucous membrane involvement, skin sloughing, may have systemic symptoms. KEY POSITIVES: Erythematous patches, bullae, epidermal necrosis, mucous membrane involvement, positive Nikolsky sign. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever >104°F (rules out toxic shock), no systemic shock (unless severe).

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rash involving >30% of body surface - toxic epidermal necrolysis, call 911

  🚨 Rash with severe systemic symptoms - fever, hypotension, tachycardia - toxic shock, call 911

  🚨 Rash with mucous membrane involvement - eyes, mouth, genitals - needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with skin sloughing - epidermal necrosis, call 911

  🚨 Rash with signs of secondary infection - fever, pus, increasing pain - needs urgent evaluation
