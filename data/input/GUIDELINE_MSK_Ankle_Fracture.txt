CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • ankle pain
  • ankle injury
  • broken ankle
  • ankle fracture
  • ankle swelling
  • can't walk on ankle
  • ankle deformity

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset during trauma - fall, twisting, or direct blow. LOCATION: Lateral malleolus (most common), medial malleolus, or posterior malleolus. DURATION: Immediate severe pain that persists. CHARACTER: SEVERE, SHARP, CONSTANT pain. AGGRAVATING: ANY weight bearing, ankle movement. RELIEVING: IMMOBILIZATION, ice, elevation, NSAIDs. TIMING: Immediate severe pain that persists until immobilization. SEVERITY: 7-10/10, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SWELLING within hours, BRUISING develops over 24-48 hours, DEFORMITY, inability to bear weight, numbness/tingling in foot (peroneal nerve compression). May have audible 'snap' or 'crack' at injury. KEY POSITIVES: Bony tenderness over fracture site, deformity, swelling, limited range of motion, positive compression test. KEY NEGATIVES: No open wound (rules out open fracture), no crepitus unless severe.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, during trauma, fall, twisting, direct blow
  Negative indicators: gradual, progressive, slow onset, days to weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: lateral malleolus, medial malleolus, posterior malleolus, ankle, outside ankle, inside ankle
  Negative indicators: knee, hip, foot, calf

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: immediate, persists, constant, ongoing
  Negative indicators: brief, minutes, hours, transient

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe, sharp, constant, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: mild, dull, intermittent, aching

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: weight bearing, ankle movement, any movement, walking
  Negative indicators: immobilization, ice, elevation, NSAIDs

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: immobilization, ice, elevation, NSAIDs, rest
  Negative indicators: weight bearing, ankle movement, walking, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: immediate, persists, constant, ongoing
  Negative indicators: delayed, intermittent, variable, episodic

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe, 7-10/10, 7, 8, 9, 10, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, moderate, 4-6/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Open wound with bone visible - open fracture, call 911

  🚨 Severe deformity with neurovascular compromise - cold, pale, pulseless foot, call 911

  🚨 Severe pain with numbness/tingling - possible compartment syndrome or nerve injury

  🚨 Elderly patients with minimal trauma - possible pathological fracture, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 High-energy trauma - possible associated injuries, needs urgent evaluation
