CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • shoulder pain
  • shoulder out of place
  • dislocated shoulder
  • shoulder injury
  • can't move shoulder
  • shoulder deformity
  • shoulder popped out

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset during trauma - fall on outstretched arm, direct blow, or forceful abduction/external rotation. LOCATION: Anterior shoulder (95% anterior dislocations), posterior shoulder (5% posterior dislocations). DURATION: Immediate severe pain that persists until reduction. CHARACTER: SEVERE, SHARP, CONSTANT pain. AGGRAVATING: ANY shoulder movement, especially abduction and external rotation. RELIEVING: NOTHING relieves pain until reduction. Patient holds arm in protective position. TIMING: Immediate severe pain that persists until reduction. SEVERITY: 8-10/10, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: OBVIOUS DEFORMITY (squared-off shoulder, prominent acromion), arm held in abduction and external rotation, inability to move shoulder, numbness/tingling in arm (axillary nerve injury), 'dead arm' sensation. May have audible 'pop' at injury. KEY POSITIVES: Obvious deformity, positive apprehension test, positive relocation test, axillary nerve deficit, prominent acromion. KEY NEGATIVES: No bony tenderness over clavicle or humerus (rules out fracture), no crepitus.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, during trauma, fall on outstretched arm, direct blow, forceful abduction, external rotation
  Negative indicators: gradual, progressive, slow onset, days to weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: anterior shoulder, posterior shoulder, shoulder, shoulder joint
  Negative indicators: elbow, wrist, neck, chest

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: immediate, persists until reduction, constant, ongoing
  Negative indicators: brief, minutes, hours, transient

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe, sharp, constant, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: mild, dull, intermittent, aching

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: shoulder movement, abduction, external rotation, any movement
  Negative indicators: reduction, immobilization, rest, treatment

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: reduction, immobilization, rest, treatment
  Negative indicators: shoulder movement, abduction, external rotation, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: immediate, persists until reduction, constant, ongoing
  Negative indicators: delayed, intermittent, variable, episodic

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe, 8-10/10, 8, 9, 10, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, moderate, 4-7/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Open wound with bone visible - open dislocation, call 911

  🚨 Severe neurovascular compromise - cold, pale, pulseless arm, call 911

  🚨 Associated fracture - humeral head or glenoid fracture, needs urgent orthopedic evaluation

  🚨 Recurrent dislocations with minimal trauma - possible chronic instability

  🚨 Elderly patients - higher risk of associated fractures and complications
