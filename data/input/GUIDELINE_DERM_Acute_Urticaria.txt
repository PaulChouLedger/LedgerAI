CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • hives
  • urticaria
  • skin rash
  • itchy bumps
  • allergic reaction
  • skin welts
  • raised rash

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within minutes to hours after exposure to trigger. LOCATION: ANYWHERE on body - face, trunk, extremities, may be localized or generalized. DURATION: Individual lesions last <24 hours, but new lesions may appear for days to weeks. CHARACTER: RAISED, WHEAL-LIKE lesions that are PRURITIC (itchy). AGGRAVATING: HEAT, stress, pressure, scratching. RELIEVING: COOL compresses, antihistamines, avoiding triggers. TIMING: Lesions appear and disappear within 24 hours, but new ones may continue. SEVERITY: 3-8/10 itching, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SEVERE ITCHING (pruritus), raised wheals with central pallor and red borders, lesions may coalesce into larger areas, may have angioedema (swelling of lips, eyes, throat). KEY POSITIVES: Raised wheals, central pallor with red borders, severe pruritus, lesions <24 hours duration. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no systemic symptoms (unless anaphylaxis), no scaling or crusting.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, minutes to hours, after exposure to trigger
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, days

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: anywhere on body, face, trunk, extremities, localized, generalized
  Negative indicators: internal organs, specific organ systems

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: individual lesions <24 hours, new lesions for days to weeks
  Negative indicators: chronic lesions, permanent lesions

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: raised, wheal-like, pruritic, itchy, central pallor, red borders
  Negative indicators: flat, non-itchy, painful, scaly

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: heat, stress, pressure, scratching
  Negative indicators: cool, rest, avoiding triggers

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: cool compresses, antihistamines, avoiding triggers
  Negative indicators: heat, scratching, stress

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: lesions appear and disappear within 24 hours, new ones may continue
  Negative indicators: constant, chronic, permanent

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 3-8/10 itching, moderate, severe
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-2/10, asymptomatic

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Difficulty breathing or swallowing - possible anaphylaxis, call 911

  🚨 Swelling of face, lips, or throat - angioedema, call 911

  🚨 Severe systemic symptoms - nausea, vomiting, dizziness - possible anaphylaxis, call 911

  🚨 Rash with fever and systemic symptoms - possible serious allergic reaction, call 911

  🚨 Rash involving mucous membranes - possible Stevens-Johnson syndrome, needs urgent evaluation
