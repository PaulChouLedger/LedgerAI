CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • facial paralysis
  • bell palsy
  • facial weakness
  • facial droop
  • facial numbness
  • facial pain
  • facial twitching

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within hours to days. LOCATION: UNILATERAL facial paralysis. DURATION: Develops over hours to days, may persist for weeks to months. CHARACTER: SUDDEN, SEVERE facial weakness or paralysis. AGGRAVATING: SPEAKING, eating, drinking. RELIEVING: STEROIDS, antiviral medications. TIMING: Sudden onset, develops over hours to days. SEVERITY: 6-9/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: FACIAL WEAKNESS, facial droop, difficulty speaking, eating, drinking, may have facial pain, may have taste changes. KEY POSITIVES: Unilateral facial weakness, facial droop, difficulty speaking, eating, drinking. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, within hours to days, immediate, acute
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset, progressive

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: unilateral facial paralysis, one side, facial, unilateral
  Negative indicators: bilateral, both sides, generalized, diffuse

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: develops over hours to days, weeks to months, prolonged, persistent
  Negative indicators: minutes, seconds, brief, transient

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sudden, severe facial weakness, paralysis, moderate to severe
  Negative indicators: mild, gradual, localized, focal

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: speaking, eating, drinking, facial movement
  Negative indicators: steroids, antiviral medications, rest, treatment

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: steroids, antiviral medications, rest, treatment
  Negative indicators: speaking, eating, drinking, facial movement

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: sudden onset, develops over hours to days, progressive
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate to severe, 6-9/10, 6, 7, 8, 9
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-5/10, severe, 10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Bilateral facial weakness - possible Guillain-Barré syndrome, call 911

  🚨 Facial weakness with other neurological deficits - possible stroke, call 911

  🚨 Facial weakness with signs of increased intracranial pressure - severe headache, vomiting, altered mental status - call 911

  🚨 Facial weakness with severe hypertension - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Facial weakness with signs of brainstem involvement - dysphagia, dysarthria, ataxia - call 911
