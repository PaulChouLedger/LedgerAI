CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • allergic reaction
  • anaphylaxis
  • severe allergy
  • throat closing
  • can't breathe
  • swelling

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: RAPID onset within minutes of exposure to allergen. LOCATION: Systemic, affecting multiple organ systems. DURATION: Minutes to hours, can be life-threatening. CHARACTER: Severe, rapidly progressive. AGGRAVATING: Continued allergen exposure. RELIEVING: Epinephrine, antihistamines, steroids. TIMING: Acute, rapid progression. SEVERITY: Severe to life-threatening (8-10/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Respiratory: stridor, wheezing, dyspnea, throat tightness. Cutaneous: urticaria, angioedema, pruritus. Cardiovascular: hypotension, tachycardia, shock. GI: nausea, vomiting, diarrhea. Neurological: altered mental status, dizziness. KEY POSITIVES: Multi-system involvement, rapid onset, known allergen exposure. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no chest pain (unless secondary to respiratory distress).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: rapid onset within minutes of exposure to allergen, acute, immediate
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: systemic, affecting multiple organ systems
  Negative indicators: localized, one side only, specific point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: minutes to hours, can be life-threatening, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe, rapidly progressive
  Negative indicators: no fever, no chest pain, mild

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: continued allergen exposure, activity, movement
  Negative indicators: epinephrine, antihistamines, steroids

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: epinephrine, antihistamines, steroids
  Negative indicators: continued allergen exposure, activity, movement

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: acute, rapid progression, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe to life-threatening, 8-10/10, 8, 9, 10
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-7/10, moderate

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Stridor or hoarse voice

  🚨 Swelling of face, lips, tongue

  🚨 Difficulty swallowing or speaking

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or wheezing

  🚨 Hypotension or shock

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea

  🚨 Generalized urticaria or rash
