CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • glomerulonephritis
  • kidney inflammation
  • renal inflammation
  • kidney disease
  • hematuria
  • proteinuria
  • kidney problems

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over days to weeks. LOCATION: BILATERAL kidney involvement. DURATION: Develops over days to weeks, may persist for weeks to months. CHARACTER: HEMATURIA, proteinuria, swelling. AGGRAVATING: DEHYDRATION, medications. RELIEVING: IMMUNOSUPPRESSANTS, supportive care. TIMING: Gradual onset, develops over days to weeks. SEVERITY: 5-8/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: HEMATURIA, proteinuria, swelling, may have hypertension, may have decreased urine output. KEY POSITIVES: Hematuria, proteinuria, swelling, elevated creatinine, elevated BUN. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe fluid overload with respiratory distress - possible pulmonary edema, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe electrolyte imbalances - confusion, seizures, cardiac arrhythmias - call 911

  🚨 Severe hypertension with neurological deficits - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of uremia - confusion, seizures, coma - call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe metabolic acidosis - deep breathing, confusion, coma - call 911
