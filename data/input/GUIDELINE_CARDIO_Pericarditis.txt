Condition: Acute Pericarditis
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: urgent

Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain
  • sharp chest pain
  • chest pain worse with breathing

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual over hours to days, often post-viral illness. LOCATION: RETROSTERNAL or left precordial (over heart). RADIATES to trapezius ridge (shoulder), neck. DURATION: Continuous for hours to days. CHARACTER: SHARP, PLEURITIC (worse with breathing), stabbing. AGGRAVATING: Deep inspiration, lying flat, cough. RELIEVING: SITTING UP and LEANING FORWARD. TIMING: Constant but fluctuates with position and breathing. SEVERITY: 6-8/10. KEY POSITIVES: Recent viral illness (URI), pericardial friction rub on exam, positional relief. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT crushing/pressure (unlike myocardial infarction ), NOT exertional.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, over hours to days, post-viral illness
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, minutes

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: retrosternal, left precordial, over heart, radiates to trapezius ridge, radiates to shoulder, radiates to neck
  Negative indicators: right side only, no radiation, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: continuous, hours to days, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, pleuritic, worse with breathing, stabbing
  Negative indicators: crushing, pressure, dull, not worse with breathing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: deep inspiration, lying flat, cough, breathing
  Negative indicators: sitting up, leaning forward, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: sitting up, leaning forward, positional relief
  Negative indicators: lying flat, deep inspiration, cough

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant, fluctuates with position, fluctuates with breathing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 6-8/10, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-5/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Fever

  🚨 Hemodynamic instability

  🚨 Large pericardial effusion

  🚨 Immunosuppression

  🚨 Trauma history

  🚨 Anticoagulation use

  🚨 Muffled heart sounds (tamponade)

  🚨 Jugular venous distension
