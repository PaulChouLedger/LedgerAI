CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • headache
  • head pain
  • head ache
  • tension headache
  • stress headache
  • head pressure
  • head discomfort

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over minutes to hours. LOCATION: BILATERAL or unilateral. DURATION: Lasts minutes to hours. CHARACTER: DULL, ACHING, PRESSURE-like pain. AGGRAVATING: STRESS, tension, poor posture. RELIEVING: REST, relaxation, medications. TIMING: Gradual onset, lasts minutes to hours. SEVERITY: 3-6/10, mild to moderate. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: MILD nausea, may have neck tension, may have stress. KEY POSITIVES: Dull pain, bilateral distribution, stress triggers. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, minutes to hours, slow onset, progressive
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, immediate, seconds

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: bilateral, unilateral, both sides, one side
  Negative indicators: localized, specific point, focal

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: minutes to hours, variable, intermittent
  Negative indicators: chronic, persistent, ongoing

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: dull, aching, pressure-like, mild
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, severe, throbbing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: stress, tension, poor posture, activity
  Negative indicators: rest, relaxation, medications, sleep

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, relaxation, medications, sleep
  Negative indicators: stress, tension, poor posture, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: gradual onset, intermittent, variable
  Negative indicators: sudden, continuous, unrelenting

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: mild to moderate, 3-6/10, 3, 4, 5, 6
  Negative indicators: severe, 7-10/10, mild, 1-2/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sudden severe headache - possible subarachnoid hemorrhage, call 911

  🚨 Headache with neck stiffness and fever - possible meningitis, call 911

  🚨 Headache with focal neurological deficits - possible stroke, call 911

  🚨 Headache with signs of increased intracranial pressure - severe headache, vomiting, altered mental status - call 911

  🚨 Headache with severe hypertension - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911
