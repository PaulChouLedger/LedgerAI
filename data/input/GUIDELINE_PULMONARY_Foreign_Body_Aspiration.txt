DIAGNOSTIC GUIDELINE: FOREIGN BODY ASPIRATION
================================================================================

Condition: Foreign Body Aspiration
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: emergent
Prevalence: uncommon
ICD-10 Code: T17.900A
SNOMED Code: 233604007
Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • choking
  • foreign body
  • something stuck in throat
  • aspiration
  • can't breathe

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden during eating or playing. LOCATION: Upper airway or bronchial tree. DURATION: Acute, persistent until removed. CHARACTER: Sudden dyspnea, choking sensation. AGGRAVATING: Continued attempts to breathe. RELIEVING: Removal of foreign body, positioning. TIMING: Acute, sudden onset. SEVERITY: Severe to life-threatening (8-10/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Sudden dyspnea, choking, coughing, stridor, cyanosis, altered mental status. KEY POSITIVES: Witnessed choking event, sudden onset, stridor, dyspnea. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no chest pain (unless secondary).

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden during eating or playing, acute, immediate
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: upper airway, bronchial tree
  Negative indicators: lower airway, chest, lungs

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: acute, persistent until removed, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sudden dyspnea, choking sensation
  Negative indicators: no fever, no chest pain, mild

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: continued attempts to breathe, activity, movement
  Negative indicators: removal of foreign body, positioning, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: removal of foreign body, positioning, rest
  Negative indicators: continued attempts to breathe, activity, movement

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: acute, sudden onset, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe to life-threatening, 8-10/10, 8, 9, 10
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-7/10, moderate

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Complete airway obstruction

  🚨 Cyanosis or respiratory arrest

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Inability to speak or cough

  🚨 Stridor or wheezing

  🚨 Signs of respiratory distress

  🚨 Unconsciousness

  🚨 Failed Heimlich maneuver
