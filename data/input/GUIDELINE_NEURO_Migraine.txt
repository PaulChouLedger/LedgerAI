CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • migraine
  • severe headache
  • head pain
  • throbbing headache
  • headache with nausea
  • visual disturbances
  • aura

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over minutes to hours. LOCATION: UNILATERAL or bilateral. DURATION: Lasts 4-72 hours. CHARACTER: THROBBING, PULSATING pain. AGGRAVATING: LIGHT, sound, movement, stress. RELIEVING: DARK, quiet room, sleep, medications. TIMING: Gradual onset, lasts 4-72 hours. SEVERITY: 6-9/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: NAUSEA, vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia, may have aura (visual, sensory, motor). KEY POSITIVES: Throbbing pain, nausea, photophobia, phonophobia, aura. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, minutes to hours, slow onset, progressive
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, immediate, seconds

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: unilateral, bilateral, one side, both sides
  Negative indicators: localized, specific point, focal

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: 4-72 hours, hours, prolonged
  Negative indicators: minutes, seconds, brief

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: throbbing, pulsating, moderate to severe, intense
  Negative indicators: dull, aching, mild, pressure-like

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: light, sound, movement, stress
  Negative indicators: dark, quiet room, sleep, medications

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: dark, quiet room, sleep, medications
  Negative indicators: light, sound, movement, stress

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: gradual onset, prolonged, episodic
  Negative indicators: sudden, continuous, unrelenting

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate to severe, 6-9/10, 6, 7, 8, 9
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-5/10, severe, 10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sudden severe headache - possible subarachnoid hemorrhage, call 911

  🚨 Headache with neck stiffness and fever - possible meningitis, call 911

  🚨 Headache with focal neurological deficits - possible stroke, call 911

  🚨 Headache with signs of increased intracranial pressure - severe headache, vomiting, altered mental status - call 911

  🚨 Headache with severe hypertension - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911
