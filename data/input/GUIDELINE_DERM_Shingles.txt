CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • shingles
  • herpes zoster
  • painful rash
  • blistering rash
  • nerve pain
  • skin blisters
  • viral rash

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over 2-3 days, often preceded by prodromal symptoms (fatigue, headache, fever). LOCATION: UNILATERAL dermatomal distribution - most commonly thoracic, trigeminal, or lumbar. DURATION: Prodrome 2-3 days, rash 2-4 weeks, pain may persist for months. CHARACTER: VESICULAR (blistering) rash with SEVERE BURNING/STINGING pain. AGGRAVATING: TOUCH, clothing, heat. RELIEVING: ANTIVIRALS (if started within 72 hours), pain medications, cool compresses. TIMING: Prodrome 2-3 days, rash develops over 2-4 weeks. SEVERITY: 6-10/10 pain, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SEVERE BURNING/STINGING pain, vesicular rash in dermatomal distribution, may have fever, malaise, headache. KEY POSITIVES: Unilateral dermatomal distribution, vesicles, severe pain, positive Tzanck smear. KEY NEGATIVES: No crossing midline, no systemic symptoms (unless disseminated), no fever >102°F.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rash involving face, especially eye - ophthalmic zoster, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash involving ear - Ramsay Hunt syndrome, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with severe systemic symptoms - disseminated zoster, call 911

  🚨 Rash with signs of secondary infection - fever, pus, increasing pain - needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with neurological deficits - possible postherpetic neuralgia, needs urgent evaluation
