DIAGNOSTIC GUIDELINE: PRINZMETAL ANGINA (VASOSPASTIC ANGINA)
================================================================================

Condition: Prinzmetal Angina (Vasospastic Angina)
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: urgent
Prevalence: rare
ICD-10 Code: I20.1
SNOMED Code: 87343002
Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain at rest
  • chest pain at night
  • early morning chest pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden, often at REST or during SLEEP. LOCATION: Retrosternal or left chest. RADIATES to arm, jaw, shoulder. DURATION: Brief, typically 5-15 minutes. CHARACTER: Squeezing, pressure, tightness. AGGRAVATING: Occurs at REST, often at NIGHT or EARLY MORNING (circadian pattern). NOT typically exertional. RELIEVING: Nitroglycerin (rapid response), calcium channel blockers. TIMING: Cyclic, often same time each day/night. SEVERITY: 6-8/10. KEY POSITIVES: Occurs at rest (especially 12am-8am), young to middle-aged, smoker, rapid relief with nitrates, ST elevation during episode that resolves. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT exertional angina, NOT persistent.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, often at rest, during sleep
  Negative indicators: gradual, with exertion, chronic

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: retrosternal, left chest, radiates to arm, radiates to jaw, radiates to shoulder
  Negative indicators: right side only, no radiation, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: brief, 5-15 minutes, short duration
  Negative indicators: prolonged, >20 minutes, continuous

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: squeezing, pressure, tightness
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, burning, crushing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: occurs at rest, often at night, early morning, circadian pattern
  Negative indicators: exertional, activity, daytime

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: nitroglycerin, rapid response, calcium channel blockers
  Negative indicators: exertion, activity, night time

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: cyclic, same time each day, same time each night
  Negative indicators: random, unpredictable, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 6-8/10, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-5/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Prolonged episode >20 minutes (may be myocardial infarction )

  🚨 Arrhythmias during episode

  🚨 Syncope

  🚨 Poor response to nitroglycerin

  🚨 Troponin elevation

  🚨 Hemodynamic instability
