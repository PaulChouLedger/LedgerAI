CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • stomach pain
  • epigastric pain
  • nausea
  • upper abdominal pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: ACUTE onset within hours to days. Triggered by NSAIDs, alcohol, or stress. LOCATION: EPIGASTRIC (upper mid-abdomen), diffuse. NO radiation. DURATION: Symptoms last hours to days. May persist if trigger continues. CHARACTER: GNAWING or BURNING quality. Discomfort more than severe pain. AGGRAVATING: WORSE WITH EATING (unlike duodenal ulcer). NSAIDs, alcohol, spicy foods worsen. RELIEVING: Antacids provide relief. Stopping NSAIDs helps. Empty stomach may feel better (opposite of duodenal ulcer). TIMING: Can be CONSTANT or EPISODIC. Often worse after meals. May persist for hours to days. SEVERITY: 4-7/10, mild to moderate discomfort. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: NAUSEA (very common), vomiting, loss of appetite, bloating, early satiety. KEY POSITIVES: Recent NSAID/alcohol use, nausea worse with eating, diffuse epigastric tenderness, antacid relief. KEY NEGATIVES: Severe LOCALIZED pain suggests ulcer. Hematemesis or melena suggests bleeding needing urgent evaluation. Weight loss suggests malignancy.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Vomiting blood (hematemesis) or coffee-ground emesis - GI bleeding, needs urgent endoscopy

  🚨 Black tarry stools (melena) - upper GI bleeding

  🚨 Severe pain with peritoneal signs - concern for perforation

  🚨 Persistent symptoms despite stopping NSAIDs and taking PPIs - needs endoscopy to rule out ulcer or malignancy
