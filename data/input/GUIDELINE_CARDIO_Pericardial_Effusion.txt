CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest discomfort
  • shortness of breath
  • chest fullness

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual, over days to weeks. LOCATION: Chest (retrosternal or precordial). DURATION: Progressive over time. CHARACTER: Dull chest discomfort, pressure, or fullness. AGGRAVATING: Lying flat. RELIEVING: Sitting up, leaning forward. TIMING: Progressive worsening. SEVERITY: 3-6/10 discomfort. KEY POSITIVES: Recent pericarditis, malignancy history, uremia (kidney failure), hypothyroidism, autoimmune disease, muffled heart sounds, dyspnea. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT hemodynamic compromise (that's tamponade), NOT Beck's triad.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, over days to weeks, slow
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, minutes

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: chest, retrosternal, precordial
  Negative indicators: right side only, no radiation, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: progressive over time, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: dull chest discomfort, pressure, fullness
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, burning, crushing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: lying flat
  Negative indicators: sitting up, leaning forward, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: sitting up, leaning forward
  Negative indicators: lying flat, activity, exertion

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: progressive worsening, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 3-6/10, 3, 4, 5, 6
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-2/10, severe, 7-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rapidly accumulating effusion

  🚨 Hypotension (suggests tamponade)

  🚨 Jugular venous distension

  🚨 Muffled heart sounds

  🚨 Pulsus paradoxus

  🚨 Large effusion on imaging

  🚨 Malignancy or uremia
