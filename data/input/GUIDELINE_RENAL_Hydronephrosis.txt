CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • hydronephrosis
  • kidney swelling
  • renal swelling
  • kidney obstruction
  • urinary obstruction
  • flank pain
  • back pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over hours to days. LOCATION: UNILATERAL or bilateral kidney involvement. DURATION: Develops over hours to days, may persist for days to weeks. CHARACTER: FLANK pain, may have decreased urine output. AGGRAVATING: MOVEMENT, urination. RELIEVING: OBSTRUCTION relief, pain medications. TIMING: Gradual onset, develops over hours to days. SEVERITY: 5-8/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: FLANK PAIN, may have decreased urine output, may have fever if infected. KEY POSITIVES: Flank pain, positive imaging, may have decreased urine output. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, progressive, hours, days, weeks
  Negative indicators: sudden, immediate

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: kidney, renal, flank, bilateral, unilateral

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: progressive

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe, decreased urine output

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: movement, urination, medications

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: pain medications

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: progressive

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe pain with fever - possible infected hydronephrosis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe infection - fever, chills, rigors - call 911

  🚨 Severe hypertension with neurological deficits - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe metabolic acidosis - deep breathing, confusion, coma - call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe electrolyte imbalances - confusion, seizures, cardiac arrhythmias - call 911
