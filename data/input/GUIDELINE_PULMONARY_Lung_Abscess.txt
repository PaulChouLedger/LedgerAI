CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • lung abscess
  • chest infection
  • fever and cough
  • productive cough with fever
  • lung infection

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual over days to weeks. LOCATION: Focal lung area, often dependent regions. DURATION: Weeks to months. CHARACTER: Productive cough, chest discomfort. AGGRAVATING: Lying flat, exertion. RELIEVING: Antibiotics, postural drainage. TIMING: Chronic, persistent. SEVERITY: Moderate to severe (6-8/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: High fever, productive cough (often foul-smelling), chest pain, dyspnea, weight loss, fatigue. KEY POSITIVES: High fever, productive cough, chest pain, weight loss. KEY NEGATIVES: No wheezing (unless reactive airway disease).

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or respiratory distress

  🚨 High fever >102°F

  🚨 Hemoptysis or blood in sputum

  🚨 Signs of sepsis or shock

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Cyanosis or oxygen saturation <90%

  🚨 Severe chest pain

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure
