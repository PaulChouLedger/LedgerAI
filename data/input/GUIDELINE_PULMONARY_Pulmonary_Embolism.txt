CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • pulmonary embolism
  • pulmonary embolism (PE)
  • blood clot in lung
  • chest pain
  • shortness of breath

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden onset. LOCATION: Chest, often pleuritic. DURATION: Acute, persistent. CHARACTER: Sudden chest pain, dyspnea. AGGRAVATING: Deep breathing, exertion. RELIEVING: Anticoagulants, oxygen. TIMING: Acute, sudden. SEVERITY: Moderate to severe (6-8/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Sudden dyspnea, chest pain, hemoptysis, syncope, leg swelling. KEY POSITIVES: Sudden onset, dyspnea, chest pain, hemoptysis. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no wheezing.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden onset, acute, immediate
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: chest, often pleuritic
  Negative indicators: localized, one area only, specific point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: acute, persistent, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sudden chest pain, dyspnea
  Negative indicators: no fever, no wheezing, sharp, stabbing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: deep breathing, exertion, activity
  Negative indicators: anticoagulants, oxygen, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: anticoagulants, oxygen, rest
  Negative indicators: deep breathing, exertion, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: acute, sudden, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate to severe, 6-8/10, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-5/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or respiratory distress

  🚨 Cyanosis or oxygen saturation <90%

  🚨 Hemoptysis

  🚨 Syncope or near-syncope

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Signs of shock

  🚨 Severe chest pain
