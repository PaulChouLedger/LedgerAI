CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • myasthenia gravis
  • muscle weakness
  • double vision
  • drooping eyelids
  • difficulty swallowing
  • breathing problems
  • fatigue

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over days to weeks. LOCATION: BILATERAL weakness, usually starting with eyes. DURATION: Progressive over days to weeks. CHARACTER: PROGRESSIVE weakness, fatigue. AGGRAVATING: ACTIVITY, stress, fatigue. RELIEVING: CHOLINESTERASE inhibitors, immunosuppressants. TIMING: Progressive over days to weeks. SEVERITY: 4-8/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: PROGRESSIVE WEAKNESS, double vision, drooping eyelids, difficulty swallowing, breathing problems, fatigue. KEY POSITIVES: Progressive weakness, double vision, drooping eyelids, difficulty swallowing, breathing problems. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe respiratory distress - possible respiratory failure, call 911

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure - difficulty breathing, cyanosis, altered mental status - call 911

  🚨 Severe hypertension with neurological deficits - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of brainstem involvement - dysphagia, dysarthria, ataxia - call 911

  🚨 Signs of increased intracranial pressure - severe headache, vomiting, altered mental status - call 911
