CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • renal failure
  • kidney failure
  • end stage renal disease
  • ESRD
  • dialysis
  • kidney problems
  • renal problems

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over months to years. LOCATION: BILATERAL kidney involvement. DURATION: Chronic progressive condition. CHARACTER: PROGRESSIVE kidney function decline. AGGRAVATING: DEHYDRATION, medications. RELIEVING: DIALYSIS, medications, fluid management. TIMING: Chronic progressive, symptoms worsen over time. SEVERITY: 6-9/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: PROGRESSIVE KIDNEY FUNCTION DECLINE, swelling, fluid retention, may have nausea, vomiting, confusion, may have electrolyte imbalances. KEY POSITIVES: Progressive kidney function decline, swelling, fluid retention, elevated creatinine, elevated BUN. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe fluid overload with respiratory distress - possible pulmonary edema, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe electrolyte imbalances - confusion, seizures, cardiac arrhythmias - call 911

  🚨 Severe hypertension with neurological deficits - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of uremia - confusion, seizures, coma - call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe metabolic acidosis - deep breathing, confusion, coma - call 911
