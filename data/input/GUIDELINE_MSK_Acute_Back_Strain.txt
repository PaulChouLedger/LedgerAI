CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • back pain
  • lower back pain
  • back injury
  • pulled back muscle
  • back spasm
  • can't move back
  • back locked up

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset during activity - lifting, twisting, bending, or sudden movement. Often during heavy lifting, awkward positioning, or sudden movement. LOCATION: Lower back (lumbar), may radiate to buttocks or thighs. DURATION: Immediate pain that may worsen over first 24-48 hours. CHARACTER: SHARP initial pain, then THROBBING and ACHING. May have muscle spasms. AGGRAVATING: BENDING, lifting, twisting, sitting, standing, coughing, sneezing. RELIEVING: REST, heat, ice, NSAIDs, muscle relaxants. TIMING: Immediate pain, stiffness increases over 24-48 hours, then gradually improves over days to weeks. SEVERITY: 4-8/10, moderate to severe with movement. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: MUSCLE SPASMS, stiffness, difficulty moving, difficulty sitting or standing, may have referred pain to buttocks or thighs. KEY POSITIVES: Paraspinal muscle tenderness, muscle spasms, limited range of motion, positive straight leg raise test (if radicular symptoms). KEY NEGATIVES: No neurological deficits, no saddle anesthesia, no bowel/bladder dysfunction, no fever, no night pain.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, during activity, lifting, twisting, bending, sudden movement, heavy lifting
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: lower back, lumbar, radiates to buttocks, radiates to thighs
  Negative indicators: upper back, neck, chest, abdomen

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: immediate pain, worsens over 24-48 hours, gradually improves, days to weeks
  Negative indicators: constant severe, unrelenting, hours only

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp initial, throbbing, aching, muscle spasms
  Negative indicators: burning, numbness, tingling, constant sharp

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: bending, lifting, twisting, sitting, standing, coughing, sneezing
  Negative indicators: rest, heat, ice, NSAIDs

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, heat, ice, NSAIDs, muscle relaxants
  Negative indicators: bending, lifting, twisting, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: immediate pain, stiffness increases, gradually improves
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, intermittent

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 4-8/10, moderate to severe, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, with movement
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe pain with neurological deficits - possible cauda equina syndrome, call 911

  🚨 Saddle anesthesia or bowel/bladder dysfunction - cauda equina syndrome, call 911

  🚨 Severe pain with fever - possible spinal infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Severe pain with night pain - possible malignancy, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Severe pain with weight loss - possible malignancy, needs urgent evaluation
