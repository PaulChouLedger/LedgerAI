CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • cough
  • chest congestion
  • bronchitis
  • chest cold
  • productive cough
  • mucus in chest

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual over days, often follows upper respiratory infection. LOCATION: Diffuse chest, trachea, bronchi. DURATION: 1-3 weeks, typically self-limiting. CHARACTER: Dry cough initially, becomes productive with yellow/green sputum. AGGRAVATING: Cold air, talking, laughing, lying flat. RELIEVING: Warm liquids, humid air, cough suppressants (limited). TIMING: Persistent throughout day, worse at night. SEVERITY: Mild to moderate (3-6/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Sore throat, nasal congestion, low-grade fever (rare), chest discomfort, fatigue. KEY POSITIVES: Productive cough, chest congestion, recent URI symptoms. KEY NEGATIVES: No high fever, no severe dyspnea, no chest pain.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual over days, often follows upper respiratory infection, progressive
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, immediate

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: diffuse chest, trachea, bronchi
  Negative indicators: localized, one side only, specific point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: 1-3 weeks, typically self-limiting, variable
  Negative indicators: chronic, persistent, ongoing

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: dry cough initially, becomes productive with yellow/green sputum, chest congestion
  Negative indicators: no high fever, no severe dyspnea, no chest pain

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: cold air, talking, laughing, lying flat
  Negative indicators: warm liquids, humid air, cough suppressants

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: warm liquids, humid air, cough suppressants
  Negative indicators: cold air, talking, laughing, lying flat

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: persistent throughout day, worse at night, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 High fever >101.5°F

  🚨 Severe shortness of breath

  🚨 Chest pain with breathing

  🚨 Blood in sputum

  🚨 Symptoms lasting >3 weeks

  🚨 Weight loss or night sweats

  🚨 Difficulty swallowing

  🚨 Neck stiffness
