Condition: Unstable Angina
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: emergent
Prevalence: common
ICD-10 Code: I20.0
SNOMED Code: 4557003
Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain
  • chest pressure
  • chest discomfort
  • angina

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: New onset OR change in pattern (more frequent, longer, lower threshold). LOCATION: RETROSTERNAL, left chest, or diffuse. RADIATES to left arm, jaw, neck, shoulder. DURATION: Usually <20 minutes but may be prolonged. CHARACTER: Pressure, tightness, squeezing, heaviness. AGGRAVATING: Occurs at REST (unlike stable angina) or with MINIMAL exertion. RELIEVING: May or may NOT respond to nitroglycerin. Rest may help. TIMING: Crescendo pattern (increasing frequency/severity). SEVERITY: 5-8/10, worsening over days. KEY POSITIVES: Dyspnea, diaphoresis, nausea. KEY NEGATIVES: NO ST elevation on electrocardiogram (ECG) (differentiates from ST-elevation myocardial infarction ).

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: new onset, change in pattern, more frequent, longer, lower threshold
  Negative indicators: stable pattern, predictable, unchanged

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: retrosternal, left chest, diffuse, radiates to left arm, radiates to jaw, radiates to neck, radiates to shoulder
  Negative indicators: right side only, no radiation, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: usually <20 minutes, may be prolonged, variable
  Negative indicators: brief, 2-10 minutes, consistent duration

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: pressure, tightness, squeezing, heaviness
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, burning, crushing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: occurs at rest, minimal exertion, unlike stable angina
  Negative indicators: only with exertion, predictable triggers, stable pattern

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: may respond to nitroglycerin, may not respond to nitroglycerin, rest may help
  Negative indicators: always responds to nitroglycerin, always responds to rest, predictable relief

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: crescendo pattern, increasing frequency, increasing severity, worsening over days
  Negative indicators: stable pattern, predictable, consistent

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 5-8/10, worsening over days, 5, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Occurs at rest or minimal exertion

  🚨 Increasing frequency or severity

  🚨 New onset angina

  🚨 Duration >20 minutes

  🚨 Poor response to nitroglycerin

  🚨 History of coronary artery disease

  🚨 Diabetes or multiple cardiac risk factors
