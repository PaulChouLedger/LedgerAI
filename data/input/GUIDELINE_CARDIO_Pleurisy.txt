Condition: Pleurisy (Pleuritis)
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: routine
Prevalence: uncommon
ICD-10 Code: R09.1
SNOMED Code: 196075003
Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain with breathing
  • sharp chest pain
  • pain when breathing deeply

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual, over hours to days, often post-viral or post-pneumonia. LOCATION: Lateral chest wall, unilateral. DURATION: Days to weeks. CHARACTER: SHARP, STABBING, PLEURITIC (worse with breathing). AGGRAVATING: DEEP INSPIRATION, cough, chest movement. RELIEVING: Splinting chest, shallow breathing, lying on affected side. TIMING: Constant but fluctuates with breathing. SEVERITY: 5-8/10 with deep breath. KEY POSITIVES: Recent respiratory infection, pleural friction rub on exam, worse with breathing/cough, unilateral. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT cardiac radiation (arm/jaw), NOT exertional, NOT crushing pressure.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, over hours to days, often post-viral, post-pneumonia
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, minutes

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: lateral chest wall, unilateral
  Negative indicators: cardiac radiation, arm, jaw, bilateral

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: days to weeks, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, stabbing, pleuritic, worse with breathing
  Negative indicators: crushing pressure, exertional, dull, not worse with breathing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: deep inspiration, cough, chest movement, breathing
  Negative indicators: splinting chest, shallow breathing, lying on affected side

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: splinting chest, shallow breathing, lying on affected side
  Negative indicators: deep inspiration, cough, chest movement

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant but fluctuates with breathing, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 5-8/10, 5, 6, 7, 8, with deep breath
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Fever >101°F (suggests pneumonia)

  🚨 Hypoxia

  🚨 Hemoptysis (coughing blood)

  🚨 Unilateral leg swelling (pulmonary embolism (PE) risk)

  🚨 Severe dyspnea

  🚨 Persistent despite treatment
