CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • tuberculosis
  • TB
  • chest infection
  • fever and cough
  • weight loss

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual over weeks to months. LOCATION: Bilateral, often upper lobes. DURATION: Chronic, months. CHARACTER: Chronic cough, chest pain. AGGRAVATING: Lying flat, exertion. RELIEVING: Antitubercular drugs. TIMING: Chronic, persistent. SEVERITY: Moderate to severe (5-8/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Chronic cough, hemoptysis, fever, night sweats, weight loss, fatigue. KEY POSITIVES: Chronic cough, hemoptysis, fever, weight loss. KEY NEGATIVES: No wheezing (unless reactive airway disease).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual over weeks to months, progressive, slow onset
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, immediate

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: bilateral, often upper lobes
  Negative indicators: unilateral, one side only, lower lobes

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: chronic, months, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: chronic cough, chest pain
  Negative indicators: no wheezing, acute, mild

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: lying flat, exertion, activity
  Negative indicators: antitubercular drugs, rest, treatment

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: antitubercular drugs, rest, treatment
  Negative indicators: lying flat, exertion, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: chronic, persistent, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate to severe, 5-8/10, 5, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or respiratory distress

  🚨 High fever >102°F

  🚨 Massive hemoptysis

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Cyanosis or oxygen saturation <90%

  🚨 Severe weight loss or cachexia

  🚨 Signs of disseminated TB
