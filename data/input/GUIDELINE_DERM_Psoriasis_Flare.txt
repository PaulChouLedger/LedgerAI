CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • psoriasis
  • skin flare
  • scaly rash
  • silver scales
  • thick skin
  • skin plaques
  • psoriatic rash

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over days to weeks, often triggered by stress, weather, or medications. LOCATION: EXTENSOR surfaces (elbows, knees), scalp, lower back, or generalized. DURATION: Chronic condition with acute flares lasting weeks to months. CHARACTER: ERYTHEMATOUS (red) plaques with SILVER SCALES. AGGRAVATING: SCRATCHING, stress, certain medications, alcohol. RELIEVING: TOPICAL steroids, moisturizers, avoiding triggers. TIMING: Chronic with acute flares, worse in winter. SEVERITY: 3-6/10 itching, mild to moderate. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: MILD ITCHING (pruritus), dry skin, scaling, may have nail changes (pitting, thickening), may have joint pain (psoriatic arthritis). KEY POSITIVES: Erythematous plaques, silver scales, extensor distribution, nail changes. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no systemic symptoms, no vesicles or bullae.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rash with fever and systemic symptoms - possible secondary infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with pus or increasing pain - secondary bacterial infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash involving >30% of body surface - severe flare, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with signs of secondary infection - fever, pus, increasing pain - needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with joint pain and swelling - psoriatic arthritis, needs urgent evaluation
