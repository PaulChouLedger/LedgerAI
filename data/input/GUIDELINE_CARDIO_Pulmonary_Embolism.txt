CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • shortness of breath
  • chest pain
  • difficulty breathing
  • sudden dyspnea

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset. LOCATION: Chest (pleuritic pain), often lateral or posterior. DURATION: Continuous dyspnea and pain. CHARACTER: PLEURITIC (sharp, worse with deep breath), dyspnea. AGGRAVATING: Deep inspiration, cough. RELIEVING: Shallow breathing. TIMING: Sudden onset, persistent. SEVERITY: Variable, 5-9/10 for pain. KEY POSITIVES: Dyspnea, tachycardia, tachypnea, hypoxia, unilateral leg swelling/pain (DVT), recent surgery/immobilization, hemoptysis (coughing blood). KEY NEGATIVES: NOT gradual onset, NOT reproducible with palpation.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, acute, immediate
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: chest, pleuritic pain, lateral, posterior
  Negative indicators: abdomen, extremities, head

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: continuous, persistent, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: pleuritic, sharp, worse with deep breath, dyspnea
  Negative indicators: dull, pressure, crushing, not worse with breathing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: deep inspiration, cough, breathing, movement
  Negative indicators: rest, shallow breathing, medication

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: shallow breathing, rest
  Negative indicators: deep inspiration, cough, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: sudden onset, persistent, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: variable, 5-9/10, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sudden onset dyspnea

  🚨 Hypoxia (low oxygen)

  🚨 Tachycardia >100 bpm

  🚨 Hemoptysis (coughing blood)

  🚨 Unilateral leg swelling or deep vein thrombosis (DVT)

  🚨 Recent surgery or immobilization

  🚨 Cancer history

  🚨 Syncope or hypotension

  🚨 Chest pain worse with breathing
