CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain
  • chest pain when swallowing
  • difficulty swallowing

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden, often during or after eating/drinking. LOCATION: RETROSTERNAL (midline chest behind breastbone). RADIATES to back occasionally. DURATION: Minutes, typically brief. CHARACTER: SQUEEZING, gripping, can mimic cardiac pain. AGGRAVATING: Swallowing (especially hot or cold liquids), eating. RELIEVING: Spontaneous resolution, nitroglycerin (can relieve, causing confusion with angina), calcium channel blockers. TIMING: Episodic, related to swallowing. SEVERITY: 5-8/10, can be severe. KEY POSITIVES: Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing), related to eating/drinking, relieved by nitrates (mimics angina!), no exertional pattern. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT exertional, NOT radiating to arm/jaw typically.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, often during eating, after eating, after drinking
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: retrosternal, midline chest, behind breastbone, radiates to back occasionally
  Negative indicators: radiates to arm, radiates to jaw, left side only

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: minutes, typically brief, short duration
  Negative indicators: continuous, unrelenting, hours

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: squeezing, gripping, can mimic cardiac pain
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, burning, no pain

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: swallowing, especially hot liquids, cold liquids, eating
  Negative indicators: spontaneous resolution, nitroglycerin, calcium channel blockers

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: spontaneous resolution, nitroglycerin, calcium channel blockers
  Negative indicators: swallowing, eating, hot liquids

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: episodic, related to swallowing
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 5-8/10, 5, 6, 7, 8, can be severe
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 New onset in elderly (rule out cardiac)

  🚨 Exertional component (suggests cardiac)

  🚨 Weight loss or dysphagia to solids (suggests stricture/cancer)

  🚨 Persistent dysphagia

  🚨 Not related to swallowing (suggests cardiac)
