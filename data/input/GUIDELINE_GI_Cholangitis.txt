CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • belly pain
  • right upper abdominal pain
  • right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain
  • upper right side pain
  • fever with abdominal pain
  • jaundice

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: ACUTE onset over hours to days, often history of gallstones or prior biliary procedures. LOCATION: Right upper quadrant (RUQ), may radiate to right shoulder/scapula. DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting hours to days. CHARACTER: DULL, ACHING, CONSTANT pain (less severe than cholecystitis alone). AGGRAVATING: No specific aggravating factors. RELIEVING: Nothing relieves pain. TIMING: CONSTANT and progressively worsening. SEVERITY: 5-8/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: CHARCOT'S TRIAD: fever + jaundice + right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain (50-70%). REYNOLDS' PENTAD: Charcot's triad + hypotension + altered mental status (7-10%, indicates severe disease). High fever with rigors, dark urine, pale stools, pruritus. KEY POSITIVES: Jaundice, high fever, right upper quadrant (RUQ) tenderness, history of gallstones. KEY NEGATIVES: Absence of jaundice makes cholangitis less likely.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Hypotension with fever and right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain - septic shock, call 911

  🚨 Altered mental status with fever and jaundice - severe sepsis, call 911

  🚨 Rigors (shaking chills) with high fever >103°F - bacteremia

  🚨 Progressive jaundice with confusion - hepatic encephalopathy risk
