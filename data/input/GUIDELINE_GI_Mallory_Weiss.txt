CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • vomiting blood
  • hematemesis
  • chest pain
  • upper abdominal pain
  • epigastric pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: ACUTE onset AFTER forceful or prolonged vomiting or retching. Classic history: vomiting non-bloody contents first, then HEMATEMESIS (blood) after repeated episodes. LOCATION: Epigastric (upper mid-abdomen) or lower chest discomfort. DURATION: Pain brief, seconds to minutes with each retch. BLEEDING typically self-limited. CHARACTER: SHARP retrosternal or epigastric pain during retching, then hematemesis. AGGRAVATING: Continued vomiting or retching worsens bleeding. RELIEVING: Cessation of vomiting allows healing. TIMING: Occurs DURING or IMMEDIATELY AFTER vomiting episodes. SEVERITY: 4-7/10, moderate. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Bright red blood or coffee-ground emesis, history of alcohol use (50%), recent gastroenteritis, pregnancy with hyperemesis. KEY POSITIVES: Hematemesis AFTER non-bloody vomiting, history of forceful retching. KEY NEGATIVES: Spontaneous hematemesis without vomiting suggests other cause (ulcer, varices).

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Large volume bright red hematemesis - arterial bleeding, call 911

  🚨 Lightheadedness, syncope, or confusion - significant blood loss

  🚨 Continued brisk bleeding despite stopping vomiting - may need endoscopy

  🚨 Known liver disease or varices - higher risk of severe bleeding
