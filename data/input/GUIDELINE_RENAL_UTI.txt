CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • UTI
  • urinary tract infection
  • bladder infection
  • urinary infection
  • burning urination
  • frequent urination
  • urinary symptoms

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over hours to days. LOCATION: LOWER urinary tract (bladder, urethra). DURATION: Develops over hours to days, may persist for days to weeks. CHARACTER: BURNING urination, frequency, urgency. AGGRAVATING: URINATION, sexual activity. RELIEVING: ANTIBIOTICS, increased fluid intake. TIMING: Gradual onset, develops over hours to days. SEVERITY: 4-7/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: BURNING URINATION, frequency, urgency, may have hematuria, may have fever if upper tract involved. KEY POSITIVES: Burning urination, frequency, urgency, positive urinalysis. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no neck stiffness, no focal neurological deficits.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Fever with urinary symptoms - possible pyelonephritis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe infection - fever, chills, rigors - call 911

  🚨 Severe hypertension with neurological deficits - possible hypertensive crisis, call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe metabolic acidosis - deep breathing, confusion, coma - call 911

  🚨 Signs of severe electrolyte imbalances - confusion, seizures, cardiac arrhythmias - call 911
