CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • pulmonary fibrosis
  • lung scarring
  • shortness of breath
  • chronic lung disease
  • interstitial lung disease

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual over months to years. LOCATION: Bilateral, diffuse. DURATION: Chronic, progressive. CHARACTER: Progressive dyspnea, dry cough. AGGRAVATING: Exertion, infections. RELIEVING: Oxygen, steroids, antifibrotics. TIMING: Chronic, progressive. SEVERITY: Moderate to severe (5-8/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Progressive dyspnea, dry cough, fatigue, weight loss, clubbing. KEY POSITIVES: Progressive dyspnea, dry cough, clubbing. KEY NEGATIVES: No chest pain (unless secondary), no wheezing (unless reactive airway disease).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual over months to years, progressive, slow onset
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, immediate

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: bilateral, diffuse
  Negative indicators: unilateral, one side only, localized

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: chronic, progressive, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: progressive dyspnea, dry cough
  Negative indicators: no chest pain, no wheezing, productive cough

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: exertion, infections, activity
  Negative indicators: oxygen, steroids, antifibrotics

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: oxygen, steroids, antifibrotics
  Negative indicators: exertion, infections, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: chronic, progressive, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate to severe, 5-8/10, 5, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or respiratory distress

  🚨 Cyanosis or oxygen saturation <90%

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Severe fatigue or weakness

  🚨 Signs of infection

  🚨 Weight loss or cachexia

  🚨 Clubbing of fingers
