CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • right upper abdominal pain
  • jaundice
  • yellow eyes
  • dark urine

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUBACUTE over days to weeks. Gradual, progressive worsening. Jaundice appears after 1-2 weeks of prodrome. LOCATION: RIGHT UPPER QUADRANT (liver area) discomfort. right upper quadrant (RUQ) tenderness with hepatomegaly (enlarged liver). DURATION: Symptoms persist for weeks. Pain is chronic/persistent (not episodic). CHARACTER: DULL, ACHING discomfort. NOT sharp. Heavy or pressure sensation in right upper quadrant (RUQ). AGGRAVATING: Fatty food intake may worsen nausea. Activity may increase fatigue. Alcohol worsens. RELIEVING: Rest helps fatigue. Avoiding fatty foods reduces nausea. Hydration helps. Position doesn't significantly change right upper quadrant (RUQ) discomfort. TIMING: CONSTANT, persistent symptoms. Progressive worsening. Not wave-like. SEVERITY: 4-7/10, mild to moderate discomfort (not severe pain). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: PROFOUND FATIGUE and malaise, JAUNDICE (yellow skin/eyes), DARK URINE (tea-colored), LIGHT-COLORED STOOLS (clay), anorexia, nausea, vomiting, LOW-GRADE FEVER. KEY POSITIVES: Scleral icterus, dark urine, profound fatigue, right upper quadrant (RUQ) tenderness, risk factors (IV drugs, unprotected sex, travel, alcohol, medications). KEY NEGATIVES: ACUTE SEVERE right upper quadrant (RUQ) pain without jaundice suggests cholecystitis. HIGH FEVER with rigors suggests cholangitis.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Altered mental status (confusion, drowsiness) - acute liver failure, call 911

  🚨 Coagulopathy (easy bruising, bleeding) - severe hepatitis with liver synthetic dysfunction

  🚨 Hypoglycemia, severe jaundice - fulminant hepatic failure, needs ICU

  🚨 Acetaminophen overdose history - potentially fatal, needs immediate N-acetylcysteine
