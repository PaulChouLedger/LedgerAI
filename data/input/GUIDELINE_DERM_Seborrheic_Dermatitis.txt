CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • seborrheic dermatitis
  • dandruff
  • scalp rash
  • oily skin
  • flaky skin
  • cradle cap
  • seborrheic eczema

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over days to weeks, often chronic with seasonal flares. LOCATION: SEBORRHEIC areas - scalp, face (eyebrows, nose, ears), chest, back. DURATION: Chronic condition with acute flares lasting days to weeks. CHARACTER: ERYTHEMATOUS (red), SCALY, OILY patches. AGGRAVATING: STRESS, weather changes, certain shampoos/soaps. RELIEVING: ANTIFUNGAL shampoos, topical steroids, avoiding triggers. TIMING: Chronic with seasonal flares, worse in winter. SEVERITY: 2-4/10 itching, mild to moderate. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: MILD ITCHING (pruritus), scaling, may have secondary bacterial infection from scratching. KEY POSITIVES: Erythematous patches, scaling, seborrheic distribution, oily appearance. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no systemic symptoms, no vesicles or bullae.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rash with fever and systemic symptoms - possible secondary infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with pus or increasing pain - secondary bacterial infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash involving >30% of body surface - severe flare, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with signs of secondary infection - fever, pus, increasing pain - needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with difficulty sleeping due to severe itching - severe flare, needs urgent evaluation
