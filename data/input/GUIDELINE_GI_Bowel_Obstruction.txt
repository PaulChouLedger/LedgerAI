CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • cramping
  • vomiting
  • no bowel movement
  • can't pass gas
  • bloating

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within 12-48 hours. Acute presentation. LOCATION: Periumbilical and diffuse (not localized to one quadrant). Cramping throughout mid-abdomen. DURATION: WAVES lasting minutes, repeating every 4-5 minutes. Entire episode lasts hours to days. CHARACTER: COLICKY CRAMPING (COMES AND GOES IN WAVES, not constant). Intermittent, wave-like quality. AGGRAVATING: EATING OR DRINKING worsens vomiting and cramping. Any oral intake intensifies symptoms. Movement during cramp worsens discomfort. RELIEVING: Vomiting provides TEMPORARY relief. Nothing to eat/drink reduces new vomiting. Position doesn't significantly help. TIMING: WAVE pattern - cramps every 4-5 minutes. Progressive worsening over time. Becomes more frequent. SEVERITY: 6-9/10 during cramps, less between waves. Moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: BILIOUS (green/yellow) VOMITING progressively more frequent, ABSOLUTE CONSTIPATION (no stool OR GAS >12-24 hours), progressive abdominal DISTENSION. Tinkling bowel sounds. KEY POSITIVES: Wave-like cramps, bilious vomiting, inability to pass flatus, distension, prior surgery. KEY NEGATIVES: Diarrhea rules out complete obstruction. CONSTANT pain with peritoneal signs suggests strangulation (surgical emergency).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, 12-48 hours, acute
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: periumbilical, diffuse, mid-abdomen, not localized
  Negative indicators: localized, one quadrant, specific point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: waves lasting minutes, repeating every 4-5 minutes, hours to days, entire episode
  Negative indicators: constant, continuous, unrelenting

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: colicky, cramping, comes and goes in waves, intermittent, wave-like
  Negative indicators: constant, steady, unrelenting

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: eating, drinking, oral intake, movement during cramp
  Negative indicators: vomiting, nothing to eat, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: vomiting, temporary relief, nothing to eat
  Negative indicators: eating, drinking, position

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: wave pattern, cramps every 4-5 minutes, progressive worsening, more frequent
  Negative indicators: constant, steady, unrelenting

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 6-9/10, moderate to severe, 6, 7, 8, 9, during cramps
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-5/10, constant severe

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe constant pain with fever and peritoneal signs - strangulated bowel, call 911

  🚨 Bloody vomit or stool - ischemic bowel, needs immediate surgery

  🚨 Severe abdominal distension with tachycardia - complete obstruction with possible perforation

  🚨 Previous bowel obstruction - higher risk of recurrence and complications
