CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • shortness of breath
  • difficulty breathing
  • wheezing
  • chest tightness
  • asthma attack
  • can't catch my breath

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden or gradual worsening over hours to days. LOCATION: Diffuse chest tightness, bilateral. DURATION: Episodes lasting minutes to hours, can be persistent. CHARACTER: Chest tightness, pressure, 'can't get air in'. AGGRAVATING: Triggers (allergens, exercise, cold air, stress, infections). RELIEVING: Bronchodilators (albuterol), sitting upright, pursed-lip breathing. TIMING: Can be episodic or persistent. SEVERITY: Variable, can be mild to life-threatening. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Wheezing (audible or auscultated), cough (dry or productive), accessory muscle use, tripod positioning, inability to speak in full sentences, cyanosis (severe cases). KEY POSITIVES: Wheezing, chest tightness, dyspnea, cough, accessory muscle use. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever (unless infection trigger), no chest pain (unless severe).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, gradual worsening, over hours to days
  Negative indicators: chronic, constant, unrelenting

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: diffuse chest tightness, bilateral
  Negative indicators: localized, one side only, specific point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: episodes lasting minutes to hours, can be persistent, variable
  Negative indicators: continuous, unrelenting, days only

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: chest tightness, pressure, can't get air in
  Negative indicators: no fever, no chest pain, sharp, stabbing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: triggers, allergens, exercise, cold air, stress, infections
  Negative indicators: bronchodilators, albuterol, sitting upright, pursed-lip breathing

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: bronchodilators, albuterol, sitting upright, pursed-lip breathing
  Negative indicators: triggers, allergens, exercise, cold air

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: can be episodic, persistent, variable
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: variable, can be mild to life-threatening, variable symptoms
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, constant

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Inability to speak in full sentences

  🚨 Cyanosis or blue lips/fingernails

  🚨 Accessory muscle use (neck/chest muscles)

  🚨 Tripod positioning (leaning forward)

  🚨 Altered mental status or confusion

  🚨 Severe dyspnea at rest

  🚨 Pulsus paradoxus (drop in blood pressure with inspiration)

  🚨 Silent chest (no wheezing despite severe obstruction)
