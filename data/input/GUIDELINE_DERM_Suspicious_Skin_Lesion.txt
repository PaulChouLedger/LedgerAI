CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • skin lump
  • skin bump
  • mole changes
  • skin lesion
  • suspicious mole
  • skin cancer
  • melanoma

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over weeks to months, often noticed by patient or family member. LOCATION: ANYWHERE on body - sun-exposed areas (face, arms, legs) most common. DURATION: Progressive over weeks to months, may change rapidly. CHARACTER: VARIABLE - may be raised, flat, pigmented, or non-pigmented. AGGRAVATING: SUN exposure, trauma, irritation. RELIEVING: AVOIDING sun exposure, protective clothing. TIMING: Progressive over weeks to months, may change rapidly. SEVERITY: 1-3/10 pain, usually asymptomatic. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Usually ASYMPTOMATIC, may have mild itching, may have bleeding or ulceration, may have color changes. KEY POSITIVES: Asymmetry, irregular borders, color variation, diameter >6mm, evolution over time (ABCDE criteria). KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no systemic symptoms, no rapid growth (unless aggressive).

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rapid growth or change in appearance - possible aggressive cancer, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Bleeding or ulceration - possible advanced cancer, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Multiple lesions or family history of skin cancer - high risk, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Lesion with satellite nodules - possible metastasis, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Lesion with regional lymphadenopathy - possible metastasis, needs urgent evaluation
