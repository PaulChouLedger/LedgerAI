CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • cold
  • upper respiratory infection
  • URI
  • nasal congestion
  • sore throat

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual over hours to days. LOCATION: Upper respiratory tract. DURATION: 3-7 days. CHARACTER: Nasal congestion, sore throat, cough. AGGRAVATING: Cold air, talking. RELIEVING: Rest, fluids, humidified air. TIMING: Acute, self-limiting. SEVERITY: Mild to moderate (3-6/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Nasal congestion, sore throat, cough, sneezing, fatigue, low-grade fever. KEY POSITIVES: Nasal congestion, sore throat, cough. KEY NEGATIVES: No chest pain, no wheezing (unless reactive airway disease).

N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual over hours to days, progressive, slow onset
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, immediate

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: upper respiratory tract
  Negative indicators: lower respiratory tract, chest, lungs

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: 3-7 days, acute, self-limiting
  Negative indicators: chronic, persistent, ongoing

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: nasal congestion, sore throat, cough
  Negative indicators: no chest pain, no wheezing, severe

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: cold air, talking, activity
  Negative indicators: rest, fluids, humidified air

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, fluids, humidified air
  Negative indicators: cold air, talking, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: acute, self-limiting, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: mild to moderate, 3-6/10, 3, 4, 5, 6
  Negative indicators: severe, 7-10/10, mild, 1-2/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 High fever >101.5°F

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or respiratory distress

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Cyanosis or oxygen saturation <90%

  🚨 Severe chest pain

  🚨 Signs of secondary bacterial infection

  🚨 Severe fatigue or weakness
