CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • fever
  • heart murmur
  • fatigue
  • night sweats

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Subacute, over days to weeks. LOCATION: Generalized systemic symptoms. DURATION: Continuous over weeks. CHARACTER: Fever, fatigue, malaise, night sweats. AGGRAVATING: None specific. RELIEVING: Antibiotics. TIMING: Progressive, worsening. SEVERITY: Variable systemic illness. KEY POSITIVES: Persistent fever, new or changing heart murmur, IVDU (injection drug use), recent dental work or procedure, prosthetic valve, splinter hemorrhages, Osler nodes, Janeway lesions, embolic phenomena (stroke, splenic infarct). KEY NEGATIVES: NOT acute onset, NOT isolated chest pain.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: subacute, over days to weeks, gradual
  Negative indicators: acute onset, sudden, minutes

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: generalized systemic symptoms
  Negative indicators: localized, one area only, chest

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: continuous over weeks, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: fever, fatigue, malaise, night sweats
  Negative indicators: isolated chest pain, no fever, no systemic symptoms

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: none specific
  Negative indicators: antibiotics, medication, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: antibiotics
  Negative indicators: activity, exertion, stress

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: progressive, worsening, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: variable systemic illness, variable
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, constant

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Persistent fever despite antibiotics

  🚨 New or changing heart murmur

  🚨 History of IVDU

  🚨 Prosthetic heart valve

  🚨 Recent dental or invasive procedure

  🚨 Embolic events (stroke, peripheral emboli)

  🚨 Heart failure symptoms

  🚨 Splinter hemorrhages or petechiae

  🚨 Septic shock
