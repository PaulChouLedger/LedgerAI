CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • upper abdominal pain
  • epigastric pain
  • stomach pain
  • pain radiating to back

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within hours to 1-2 days. Often triggered by ALCOHOL BINGE or HEAVY FATTY MEAL (gallstones). LOCATION: Epigastric (upper mid-abdomen) and periumbilical. RADIATES STRAIGHT THROUGH TO THE BACK in 'boring' or 'band-like' pattern - highly characteristic. May radiate to left upper quadrant. DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting hours to days (not episodic). CHARACTER: DEEP ACHING or KNIFE-LIKE. Described as boring, penetrating, or band-like across upper abdomen. AGGRAVATING: WORSE WHEN LYING FLAT. Pain intensifies in supine position. RELIEVING: BETTER WHEN SITTING UP AND LEANING FORWARD. Patients assume 'fetal position' or lean forward for relief. TIMING: CONSTANT and unrelenting. Persists continuously (not waves). SEVERITY: 8-10/10, very severe and debilitating. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: PERSISTENT NAUSEA AND VOMITING (severe, not relieved by vomiting), anorexia, low-grade fever, abdominal distension. KEY POSITIVES: Epigastric→back radiation, relief with leaning forward, alcohol/fatty meal trigger, persistent vomiting. KEY NEGATIVES: Pain NOT radiating to back is atypical. Diarrhea uncommon initially. right lower quadrant (RLQ) pain suggests appendicitis not pancreatitis.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, hours, 1-2 days, alcohol binge, heavy fatty meal, gallstones
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: epigastric, upper mid-abdomen, periumbilical, radiates to back, left upper quadrant, boring pattern, band-like
  Negative indicators: right lower quadrant, RLQ, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: multiple days, hours, continuous, days, constant, persistent, several hours
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: deep aching, knife-like, boring, penetrating, band-like, severe
  Negative indicators: mild, crampy, dull

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: lying flat, supine position, lying down
  Negative indicators: sitting up, leaning forward, fetal position

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: sitting up, leaning forward, fetal position
  Negative indicators: lying flat, supine, medication

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant, unrelenting, persists continuously, not waves
  Negative indicators: waves, intermittent, episodic

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 9, 8, 10, 8-10/10, severe, debilitating
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, 1-5/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe abdominal pain with hypotension or tachycardia - severe pancreatitis with third-spacing, needs ICU

  🚨 Respiratory distress or low oxygen saturation - ARDS complication, call 911

  🚨 Altered mental status, severe dehydration - severe systemic inflammation

  🚨 Grey-Turner sign (flank bruising) or Cullen sign (periumbilical bruising) - hemorrhagic pancreatitis, very severe
