CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • stomach pain
  • epigastric pain
  • burning stomach
  • upper abdominal pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL or chronic/recurrent over weeks to months. Not sudden. LOCATION: EPIGASTRIC, midline upper abdomen. No radiation typically. DURATION: Episodes last minutes to hours. May be constant in severe cases. CHARACTER: BURNING or GNAWING quality. Aching or hunger-like discomfort. AGGRAVATING: DUODENAL ulcers worsen 2-5 hours after meals (empty stomach). GASTRIC ulcers worsen WITH food. NSAIDs worsen. RELIEVING: DUODENAL ulcers improve WITH food and ANTACIDS. GASTRIC ulcers may improve with antacids but worsen with food. TIMING: EPISODIC with meal-related pattern. NOCTURNAL pain (wakes patient at night) is characteristic. DUODENAL=better with food, GASTRIC=worse with food. SEVERITY: 5-8/10, moderate. Not usually severe unless perforation. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Nausea, bloating, early satiety. NO vomiting unless obstruction. KEY POSITIVES: Meal-related pattern, antacid relief, nocturnal pain, NSAID use, H. pylori history. KEY NEGATIVES: SUDDEN SEVERE pain suggests perforation. Hematemesis (vomiting blood) or melena (black tarry stools) indicates bleeding ulcer needing urgent care. Weight loss suggests gastric cancer.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, chronic, recurrent, weeks to months
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, hours

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: epigastric, midline upper abdomen, upper abdomen
  Negative indicators: radiates, lower abdomen, sides

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: minutes to hours, episodes, constant in severe cases
  Negative indicators: days, weeks, continuous

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: burning, gnawing, aching, hunger-like, discomfort
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, crampy

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: duodenal ulcers worsen 2-5 hours after meals, gastric ulcers worsen with food, NSAIDs, empty stomach
  Negative indicators: food for duodenal, antacids

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: duodenal ulcers improve with food, antacids, food for duodenal
  Negative indicators: food for gastric, NSAIDs

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: episodic, meal-related pattern, nocturnal, wakes at night
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 5-8/10, moderate, 5, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: severe, 9-10/10, mild

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sudden severe epigastric pain with peritoneal signs - perforated ulcer, call 911

  🚨 Vomiting blood or coffee-ground emesis - bleeding ulcer, needs urgent endoscopy

  🚨 Black tarry stools (melena) - GI bleeding

  🚨 Weight loss with early satiety - gastric cancer, needs urgent endoscopy
