CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • hip pain
  • hip injury
  • broken hip
  • hip fracture
  • can't walk
  • hip deformity
  • fell on hip

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset during trauma - fall, direct blow, or high-energy impact. Common in elderly with osteoporosis. LOCATION: Femoral neck, intertrochanteric, or subtrochanteric region. DURATION: Immediate severe pain that persists. CHARACTER: SEVERE, SHARP, CONSTANT pain. AGGRAVATING: ANY weight bearing, hip movement, leg movement. RELIEVING: NOTHING relieves pain until immobilization. Patient cannot bear weight. TIMING: Immediate severe pain that persists until surgical fixation. SEVERITY: 8-10/10, severe and unrelenting. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: UNABLE TO BEAR WEIGHT, leg appears shortened and externally rotated, severe pain with any movement, may have audible 'snap' or 'crack' at injury. KEY POSITIVES: Bony tenderness over hip, deformity, inability to bear weight, positive log roll test, positive heel strike test. KEY NEGATIVES: No open wound (rules out open fracture), no crepitus unless severe.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, during trauma, fall, direct blow, high-energy impact
  Negative indicators: gradual, progressive, slow onset, days to weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: femoral neck, intertrochanteric, subtrochanteric, hip, hip joint
  Negative indicators: knee, ankle, thigh, pelvis

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: immediate, persists, constant, ongoing
  Negative indicators: brief, minutes, hours, transient

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: severe, sharp, constant, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: mild, dull, intermittent, aching

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: weight bearing, hip movement, leg movement, any movement
  Negative indicators: immobilization, rest, surgical fixation, treatment

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: immobilization, surgical fixation, rest, treatment
  Negative indicators: weight bearing, hip movement, leg movement, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: immediate, persists, constant, ongoing
  Negative indicators: delayed, intermittent, variable, episodic

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe, 8-10/10, 8, 9, 10, unrelenting
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, moderate, 4-7/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Open wound with bone visible - open fracture, call 911

  🚨 Severe pain with neurovascular compromise - cold, pale, pulseless leg, call 911

  🚨 Elderly patients with minimal trauma - possible pathological fracture, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 High-energy trauma - possible associated injuries, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Severe pain with inability to move leg - possible nerve injury or compartment syndrome
