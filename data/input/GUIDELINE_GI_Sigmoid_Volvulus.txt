CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • belly pain
  • left lower abdominal pain
  • lower left side pain
  • severe constipation
  • distended abdomen
  • can't pass gas

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: ACUTE onset over hours to days. More common in elderly, institutionalized, or chronic constipation patients. LOCATION: Left lower quadrant (LLQ) or diffuse lower abdomen. DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting hours to days. CHARACTER: CRAMPY, COLICKY pain initially, becoming CONSTANT and SEVERE if ischemia develops. AGGRAVATING: Eating or drinking worsens distension and pain. RELIEVING: NOTHING relieves pain. Vomiting may provide brief relief of distension. TIMING: Initially INTERMITTENT crampy pain, becomes CONSTANT as obstruction persists. SEVERITY: 6-9/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Marked abdominal distension (asymmetric), absolute constipation (obstipation), inability to pass flatus, nausea and vomiting (may be feculent if prolonged). Tympanic distended abdomen. KEY POSITIVES: Massive asymmetric distension, obstipation, elderly patient, chronic constipation history. KEY NEGATIVES: Ability to pass large amounts of flatus or stool rules out complete obstruction.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sudden severe pain with rigid abdomen - perforation with peritonitis, call 911

  🚨 Fever with severe pain - ischemic or necrotic bowel

  🚨 Hypotension or altered mental status - septic shock, call 911

  🚨 Bloody or dark red stools - mucosal ischemia
