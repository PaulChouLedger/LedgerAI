CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • palpitations
  • irregular heartbeat
  • fast heartbeat
  • chest fluttering
  • racing heart

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden or gradual, can be paroxysmal (comes and goes). LOCATION: Chest (palpitations), generalized. DURATION: Variable (minutes to persistent). CHARACTER: IRREGULAR, RAPID palpitations, "fluttering" or "quivering" in chest. AGGRAVATING: Caffeine, alcohol, stress, dehydration. RELIEVING: Rest, sometimes spontaneous. TIMING: Paroxysmal (episodic) or persistent. SEVERITY: Variable discomfort. KEY POSITIVES: Dyspnea, lightheadedness, fatigue, irregularly irregular pulse on exam, chest discomfort. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT regular rapid heartbeat (SVT), NOT slow irregular (heart block).

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, gradual, paroxysmal, comes and goes
  Negative indicators: chronic, constant, unrelenting

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: chest, palpitations, generalized
  Negative indicators: localized, one area only, extremities

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: variable, minutes to persistent, episodic
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, hours only

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: irregular, rapid palpitations, fluttering, quivering
  Negative indicators: regular, slow, steady

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: caffeine, alcohol, stress, dehydration
  Negative indicators: rest, medication, spontaneous

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, sometimes spontaneous
  Negative indicators: caffeine, alcohol, stress

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: paroxysmal, episodic, persistent
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: variable discomfort, variable
  Negative indicators: severe, mild, constant

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Hemodynamic instability (hypotension)

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or chest pain

  🚨 Syncope or near-syncope

  🚨 Heart rate >150 bpm

  🚨 Signs of heart failure

  🚨 History of stroke or transient ischemic attack (TIA)

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Active chest pain
