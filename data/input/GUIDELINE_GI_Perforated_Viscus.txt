CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • severe abdominal pain
  • sudden severe pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN, DRAMATIC onset within minutes. Acute, catastrophic presentation. LOCATION: Initially EPIGASTRIC (perforated ulcer) or localized, then becomes DIFFUSE as peritonitis develops. May have shoulder pain (diaphragmatic irritation). DURATION: CONSTANT from onset. Unrelenting, persists continuously. CHARACTER: SUDDEN, SEVERE, SHARP pain described as 'STABBED' or 'KICKED'. Excruciating, worst pain imaginable. AGGRAVATING: ANY MOVEMENT worsens pain dramatically. Breathing, coughing, talking all worsen. Jarring or bumps unbearable. RELIEVING: NOTHING helps. Patient lies PERFECTLY STILL. Position doesn't relieve. Requires emergency surgery. TIMING: CONSTANT and unrelenting from onset. Progressive worsening as peritonitis spreads. SEVERITY: 10/10, maximal severity. Catastrophic pain. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Nausea, vomiting, inability to pass gas, fever develops after 6-12 hours. BOARD-LIKE RIGID ABDOMEN, absent bowel sounds. KEY POSITIVES: Rigid abdomen, severe rebound, patient perfectly still, sudden onset, prior ulcer/NSAID use. KEY NEGATIVES: SOFT abdomen rules out. Gradual onset suggests alternative. SURGICAL EMERGENCY - call 911.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, dramatic, minutes, acute, catastrophic
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, hours, slow

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: epigastric, initially localized, then diffuse, shoulder pain, diaphragmatic irritation
  Negative indicators: constant location, one quadrant only

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: constant from onset, unrelenting, persists continuously
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sudden, severe, sharp, stabbed, kicked, excruciating, worst pain imaginable
  Negative indicators: mild, dull, crampy, moderate

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: any movement, breathing, coughing, talking, jarring, bumps
  Negative indicators: lying still, rest, position changes

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: nothing helps, lying perfectly still
  Negative indicators: position, medication, movement

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant, unrelenting, progressive worsening, from onset
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 10/10, maximal, catastrophic, 10
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, 1-9/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 SURGICAL EMERGENCY - rigid abdomen with severe pain - call 911 immediately

  🚨 Hypotension or tachycardia - septic shock from peritonitis

  🚨 Free air under diaphragm on x-ray - confirmatory for perforation

  🚨 ANY delay in treatment increases mortality - needs emergency surgery
