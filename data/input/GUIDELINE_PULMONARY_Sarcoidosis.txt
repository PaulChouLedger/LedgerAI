CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • sarcoidosis
  • chest pain
  • shortness of breath
  • lung disease
  • fatigue

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Gradual over months to years. LOCATION: Bilateral, diffuse. DURATION: Chronic, persistent. CHARACTER: Progressive dyspnea, chest pain. AGGRAVATING: Exertion, infections. RELIEVING: Steroids, immunosuppressants. TIMING: Chronic, progressive. SEVERITY: Moderate to severe (5-8/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Progressive dyspnea, chest pain, fatigue, weight loss, skin lesions. KEY POSITIVES: Progressive dyspnea, chest pain, fatigue. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever (unless infection), no wheezing (unless reactive airway disease).

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or respiratory distress

  🚨 Cyanosis or oxygen saturation <90%

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure

  🚨 Altered mental status

  🚨 Severe fatigue or weakness

  🚨 Signs of infection

  🚨 Weight loss or cachexia

  🚨 Signs of cardiac involvement
