CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • heartburn
  • chest pain
  • burning
  • acid reflux
  • regurgitation

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL, CHRONIC/RECURRENT over weeks to months (not acute). Often after meals or when lying down. LOCATION: RETROSTERNAL (behind breastbone) and EPIGASTRIC. BURNING rises from stomach toward throat. No radiation to arms/jaw. DURATION: Episodes last minutes to hours. Can be constant if severe reflux. May persist after meals. CHARACTER: BURNING sensation (heartburn). NOT sharp or pressure-like (would suggest cardiac). Acid rising sensation. AGGRAVATING: WORSE AFTER MEALS, when LYING DOWN, or BENDING OVER. Spicy foods, caffeine, alcohol, large meals trigger. RELIEVING: ANTACIDS provide quick relief. SITTING UPRIGHT helps. Elevation of head during sleep helps. TIMING: EPISODIC - worse after meals, at night when lying flat. Pattern repeats with dietary triggers. SEVERITY: 3-6/10, mild to moderate discomfort. Not severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SOUR TASTE (acid regurgitation), belching, dysphagia (if chronic), chronic cough, hoarseness. KEY POSITIVES: Antacid relief, worse when lying down, chronic pattern, dietary triggers. KEY NEGATIVES: NEW SEVERE chest pain requires cardiac workup FIRST. Dysphagia with weight loss suggests stricture/cancer. Hematemesis suggests ulcer.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: chronic, recurrent, months, years
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, hours

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: epigastric, substernal, chest, upper abdomen
  Negative indicators: lower abdomen, RLQ, LLQ
  Anatomical type: upper_central

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: chronic, episodic, minutes to hours
  Negative indicators: constant, days

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: burning, heartburn, acid, rising sensation
  Negative indicators: sharp, stabbing, pressure-like, crushing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: lying down, bending, large meals, spicy foods
  Negative indicators: movement, coughing

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: sitting up, antacids, proton pump inhibitors
  Negative indicators: lying still, movement

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: after meals, at night, episodic
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelated to meals

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: mild, moderate, 3, 4, 5, 6
  Negative indicators: severe, 8, 9, 10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 New severe chest pain - rule out cardiac causes (myocardial infarction (MI), angina) FIRST before attributing to gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)

  🚨 Dysphagia (difficulty swallowing) with weight loss - esophageal stricture or cancer, needs endoscopy

  🚨 Vomiting blood or black tarry stools - GI bleeding from severe esophagitis or ulcer

  🚨 Symptoms not responding to PPI therapy - consider alternative diagnosis or Barrett's esophagus
