================================================================================

Condition: Incarcerated Inguinal/Femoral Hernia
Category: N/A
Urgency Level: urgent
Prevalence: uncommon
ICD-10 Code: K40.30
SNOMED Code: 236477004
Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • groin pain
  • groin bulge
  • abdominal pain
  • lower abdominal pain
  • lump in groin
  • painful bulge

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: ACUTE worsening over hours, often history of known hernia that was previously reducible. LOCATION: Groin (inguinal or femoral region), may have lower abdominal pain if bowel involved. DURATION: CONSTANT pain since hernia became irreducible. CHARACTER: SHARP, CONSTANT pain at hernia site, becomes SEVERE if strangulation occurs. AGGRAVATING: Standing, straining, coughing worsen pain. Cannot reduce (push back) the hernia. RELIEVING: Lying down may decrease discomfort slightly but hernia remains out. NOTHING fully relieves pain. TIMING: CONSTANT and progressively worsening if strangulation developing. SEVERITY: 6-9/10 if incarcerated, 9-10/10 if strangulated. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Tender, firm, non-reducible bulge in groin. Nausea, vomiting if bowel obstruction. Overlying skin erythema if strangulation. KEY POSITIVES: Irreducible groin mass, known hernia history, sudden change from reducible to irreducible. KEY NEGATIVES: Easily reducible bulge suggests simple hernia without incarceration.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: acute, worsening over hours, sudden change, previously reducible
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: groin, inguinal, femoral, lower abdominal, hernia site
  Negative indicators: upper abdomen, chest, back

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: constant, persistent, continuous, since irreducible
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: stabbing, sharp, knife-like, unrelenting, constant, steady, severe if strangulation
  Negative indicators: mild, dull, crampy

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: standing, straining, coughing, movement
  Negative indicators: lying down, rest, reducing hernia

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: lying down, slight decrease
  Negative indicators: nothing fully relieves, hernia remains out, cannot reduce

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: all the time, continuous, progressive, worsening, getting worse, constant
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 9, 6-9/10, 9-10/10, severe, if strangulated
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-5/10, moderate

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Overlying skin changes (red, dusky, necrotic) - strangulation, call 911

  🚨 Severe pain with fever - infected/necrotic bowel

  🚨 Vomiting with abdominal distension - bowel obstruction

  🚨 Sudden relief of pain - possible bowel perforation, call 911
