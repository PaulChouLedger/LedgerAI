CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • ankle pain
  • twisted ankle
  • rolled ankle
  • ankle injury
  • sprained ankle
  • ankle swelling
  • can't walk on ankle

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset during activity - twisting, rolling, or landing awkwardly. Usually during sports, walking on uneven surface, or stepping off curb. LOCATION: Lateral ankle (outside) most common (85%), medial ankle (inside) less common, anterior ankle possible. DURATION: Immediate pain that may worsen over first 24-48 hours. CHARACTER: SHARP initial pain, then THROBBING and ACHING. AGGRAVATING: WEIGHT BEARING, walking, ankle movement, especially inversion (rolling inward). RELIEVING: REST, ICE, elevation, NSAIDs help. TIMING: Immediate pain that peaks at 24-48 hours, then gradually improves over days to weeks. SEVERITY: 4-8/10 initially, improves with rest. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SWELLING within hours (lateral ankle), BRUISING (ecchymosis) develops over 24-48 hours, STIFFNESS, difficulty walking or bearing weight. May have 'popping' or 'snapping' sound at injury. KEY POSITIVES: Lateral ankle tenderness, swelling, bruising, positive anterior drawer test, positive talar tilt test. KEY NEGATIVES: No bony tenderness over malleoli (rules out fracture), no deformity, no crepitus.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, during activity, twisting, rolling, landing awkwardly
  Negative indicators: gradual, progressive, slow onset, days to weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: lateral ankle, outside ankle, medial ankle, inside ankle, anterior ankle
  Negative indicators: knee, hip, foot, calf

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: immediate, 24-48 hours, days to weeks, gradual improvement
  Negative indicators: seconds, minutes, chronic, months

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, throbbing, aching, initial pain
  Negative indicators: dull, burning, numbness, tingling

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: weight bearing, walking, ankle movement, inversion, rolling inward
  Negative indicators: rest, ice, elevation, NSAIDs

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest, ice, elevation, NSAIDs, immobilization
  Negative indicators: weight bearing, walking, ankle movement, inversion

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: immediate, peaks at 24-48 hours, gradual improvement, days to weeks
  Negative indicators: delayed, chronic, intermittent, constant

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 4-8/10, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, improves with rest
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Inability to bear weight immediately after injury - possible fracture, needs X-ray

  🚨 Severe deformity or obvious fracture - obvious ankle fracture, call 911

  🚨 Numbness or tingling in foot - possible nerve injury or compartment syndrome

  🚨 Cold, pale, or blue foot - possible vascular injury, call 911

  🚨 Open wound with bone visible - open fracture, call 911
