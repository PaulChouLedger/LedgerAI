CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • diarrhea
  • vomiting
  • stomach pain
  • cramping
  • stomach bug
  • food poisoning

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within hours to 1-2 days. Acute presentation. Often follows sick contacts or contaminated food/water. LOCATION: PERIUMBILICAL or DIFFUSE throughout abdomen. NOT localized to one quadrant. Cramping moves around. DURATION: Episodes last hours to days. Individual cramps last seconds to minutes. Typically resolves within 3-7 days. CHARACTER: CRAMPY, DIFFUSE pain. INTERMITTENT (comes and goes in waves), NOT constant. Dull cramping quality. AGGRAVATING: Eating may worsen cramping initially. Movement during cramp uncomfortable. Bowel movements trigger cramping. RELIEVING: Defecation provides TEMPORARY relief. Bowel movements reduce cramping. Symptoms improve with hydration and rest. TIMING: INTERMITTENT waves of cramping with bowel movements. Cramps come and go. NOT constant. SEVERITY: 3-6/10, mild to moderate cramping. DIARRHEA is PREDOMINANT symptom (more prominent than pain). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: DIARRHEA (3+ watery stools/day) as main symptom, NAUSEA AND VOMITING (often BEFORE or WITH diarrhea), low-grade fever or none, hyperactive bowel sounds. APPETITE often PRESERVED (unlike appendicitis with severe anorexia). KEY POSITIVES: Diarrhea predominant, diffuse cramping, sick contacts, appetite preserved, hyperactive bowel sounds. KEY NEGATIVES: Pain MIGRATING to right lower quadrant (RLQ) suggests appendicitis. SEVERE LOCALIZED tenderness with guarding rules out. Pain WITHOUT diarrhea uncommon.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, hours, 1-2 days, acute, sick contacts, contaminated food
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, weeks

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: periumbilical, diffuse, throughout abdomen, moves around
  Negative indicators: localized, one quadrant, right lower quadrant, RLQ

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: hours to days, seconds to minutes, resolves within 3-7 days, episodes
  Negative indicators: constant, persistent, weeks

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: crampy, diffuse, intermittent, dull cramping, comes and goes
  Negative indicators: constant, sharp, stabbing, severe

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: eating, movement during cramp, bowel movements, straining
  Negative indicators: rest, defecation, hydration

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: defecation, bowel movements, passing gas, hydration, rest
  Negative indicators: eating, movement

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: intermittent, waves, comes and goes, not constant
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 3-6/10, mild, moderate, 3, 4, 5, 6
  Negative indicators: severe, 7-10/10, excruciating

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dehydration (dry mucous membranes, decreased urine output, orthostatic hypotension) - needs IV fluids

  🚨 Bloody diarrhea with high fever >102°F - invasive bacterial infection (Shigella, Salmonella, E. coli), may need antibiotics

  🚨 Severe abdominal pain with peritoneal signs - suggests perforation or alternative diagnosis, not simple gastroenteritis

  🚨 Immunocompromised patients or infants - higher risk of severe dehydration and complications
