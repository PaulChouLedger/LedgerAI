CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chronic cough
  • productive cough
  • bronchiectasis
  • recurrent lung infections
  • copious sputum

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Chronic, progressive over months to years. LOCATION: Bilateral, often lower lobes. DURATION: Chronic, persistent with acute exacerbations. CHARACTER: Chronic productive cough with copious sputum. AGGRAVATING: Infections, lying flat, morning. RELIEVING: Postural drainage, chest physiotherapy, antibiotics. TIMING: Worse in morning, improves with postural drainage. SEVERITY: Moderate to severe (5-8/10). ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Chronic productive cough, recurrent infections, hemoptysis, dyspnea, fatigue, weight loss. KEY POSITIVES: Chronic productive cough, recurrent infections, copious sputum. KEY NEGATIVES: No chest pain (unless infection), no wheezing (unless reactive airway disease).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: chronic, progressive over months to years, slow onset
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, immediate

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: bilateral, often lower lobes
  Negative indicators: unilateral, one side only, upper lobes

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: chronic, persistent with acute exacerbations, ongoing
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: chronic productive cough with copious sputum, productive cough
  Negative indicators: no chest pain, no wheezing, dry cough

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: infections, lying flat, morning
  Negative indicators: postural drainage, chest physiotherapy, antibiotics

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: postural drainage, chest physiotherapy, antibiotics
  Negative indicators: infections, lying flat, morning

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: worse in morning, improves with postural drainage, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: moderate to severe, 5-8/10, 5, 6, 7, 8
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Massive hemoptysis

  🚨 Severe dyspnea

  🚨 High fever with purulent sputum

  🚨 Weight loss or cachexia

  🚨 Recurrent pneumonias

  🚨 Clubbing of fingers

  🚨 Signs of respiratory failure

  🚨 Altered mental status
