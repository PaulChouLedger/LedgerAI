--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain
  • shortness of breath
  • fatigue
  • palpitations

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Days to weeks, often follows viral illness. LOCATION: Chest (retrosternal or left-sided). DURATION: Continuous over days. CHARACTER: Dull ache, pressure, or sharp pleuritic pain. AGGRAVATING: Exertion. RELIEVING: Rest. TIMING: Progressive over days. SEVERITY: Variable, 4-7/10. KEY POSITIVES: Recent viral illness (URI, GI illness) 1-3 weeks prior, dyspnea, fatigue, palpitations, fever, young patient. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT typical exertional angina pattern, NOT elderly (usually younger patients).

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: days to weeks, often follows viral illness, gradual
  Negative indicators: sudden, acute, minutes

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: chest, retrosternal, left-sided
  Negative indicators: right side only, no radiation, localized point

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: continuous over days, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: dull ache, pressure, sharp pleuritic pain
  Negative indicators: crushing, burning, no pain

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: exertion, activity
  Negative indicators: rest, medication, position

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: rest
  Negative indicators: exertion, activity, stress

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: progressive over days, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: variable, 4-7/10, 4, 5, 6, 7
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, severe, 8-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea or heart failure symptoms

  🚨 Syncope or presyncope

  🚨 Chest pain with troponin elevation

  🚨 Arrhythmias

  🚨 Hypotension or shock

  🚨 Recent viral illness

  🚨 Young patient with myocardial infarction (MI)-like presentation

  🚨 Family history of sudden cardiac death
