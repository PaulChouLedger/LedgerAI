CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • eczema
  • atopic dermatitis
  • skin flare
  • itchy skin
  • dry skin rash
  • skin inflammation
  • dermatitis flare

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: GRADUAL onset over days to weeks, often triggered by stress, weather, or irritants. LOCATION: FLEXURAL areas (elbows, knees, neck), face, hands, or generalized. DURATION: Chronic condition with acute flares lasting days to weeks. CHARACTER: ERYTHEMATOUS (red), PRURITIC (itchy), DRY, SCALY skin. AGGRAVATING: SCRATCHING, heat, stress, certain fabrics, soaps. RELIEVING: MOISTURIZERS, topical steroids, antihistamines, avoiding triggers. TIMING: Chronic with acute flares, worse at night. SEVERITY: 4-8/10 itching, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: SEVERE ITCHING (pruritus), dry skin, scaling, may have lichenification (thickened skin), may have secondary infection from scratching. KEY POSITIVES: Erythema, pruritus, scaling, lichenification, flexural distribution. KEY NEGATIVES: No fever, no systemic symptoms, no vesicles or bullae (unless secondary infection).

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: gradual, days to weeks, triggered by stress, weather, irritants
  Negative indicators: sudden, immediate, chronic without flares

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: flexural areas, elbows, knees, neck, face, hands, generalized
  Negative indicators: internal organs, specific organ systems

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: chronic condition, acute flares, days to weeks
  Negative indicators: acute only, permanent resolution

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: erythematous, red, pruritic, itchy, dry, scaly
  Negative indicators: moist, non-itchy, smooth, normal skin

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: scratching, heat, stress, certain fabrics, soaps
  Negative indicators: moisturizers, cool, avoiding triggers

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: moisturizers, topical steroids, antihistamines, avoiding triggers
  Negative indicators: scratching, heat, stress, irritants

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: chronic with acute flares, worse at night
  Negative indicators: constant, only during day, intermittent

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 4-8/10 itching, moderate, severe
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-3/10, asymptomatic

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Rash with fever and systemic symptoms - possible secondary infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with pus or increasing pain - secondary bacterial infection, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash involving >30% of body surface - severe flare, needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with signs of secondary infection - fever, pus, increasing pain - needs urgent evaluation

  🚨 Rash with difficulty sleeping due to severe itching - severe flare, needs urgent evaluation
