Condition: Pneumothorax (Collapsed Lung)

Sex: both

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • sudden chest pain
  • sudden shortness of breath
  • difficulty breathing
  • sharp chest pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset. LOCATION: UNILATERAL chest pain (left or right). DURATION: Continuous until treated. CHARACTER: SHARP, PLEURITIC (worse with breathing). AGGRAVATING: Deep inspiration, cough. RELIEVING: Shallow breathing, sitting upright. TIMING: Sudden onset, persistent. SEVERITY: 7-10/10 for pain and dyspnea. KEY POSITIVES: Tall, thin young male (spontaneous), recent trauma or procedure, decreased breath sounds on affected side, hyperresonance, dyspnea. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT bilateral, NOT gradual onset, NOT cardiac radiation pattern.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden onset, acute, immediate
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: unilateral chest pain, left or right, one side
  Negative indicators: bilateral, cardiac radiation pattern, both sides

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: continuous until treated, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, pleuritic, worse with breathing
  Negative indicators: dull, pressure, crushing, not worse with breathing

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: deep inspiration, cough, breathing
  Negative indicators: shallow breathing, sitting upright, rest

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: shallow breathing, sitting upright
  Negative indicators: deep inspiration, cough, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: sudden onset, persistent, continuous
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, comes and goes

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 7-10/10, 7, 8, 9, 10, for pain and dyspnea
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-6/10, moderate

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Severe dyspnea

  🚨 Hypoxia

  🚨 Hypotension (tension pneumothorax)

  🚨 Tracheal deviation (tension)

  🚨 Jugular venous distension (tension)

  🚨 Recent trauma or central line placement

  🚨 Mechanical ventilation

  🚨 Absent breath sounds on one side
