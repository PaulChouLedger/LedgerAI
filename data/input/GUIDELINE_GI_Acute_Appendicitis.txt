CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • belly pain
  • stomach pain
  • right lower abdominal pain
  • right lower quadrant (RLQ) pain
  • lower right side pain

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: SUDDEN onset within 6-48 hours (acute, NOT chronic). LOCATION: Pain MIGRATES from periumbilical to right lower quadrant (RLQ) over 12-24 hours - highly specific migration pattern. Localizes to McBurney's point in right lower quadrant (RLQ). DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting hours to days (not episodic). CHARACTER: SHARP and CONSTANT (NOT crampy or intermittent). AGGRAVATING: WORSENS WITH MOVEMENT - patients can't jump, cough, or tolerate car bumps. Lying still required. RELIEVING: Lying motionless provides slight relief. NOTHING fully relieves pain. Movement worsens it. TIMING: CONSTANT and progressively worsening over time (not waves). SEVERITY: 7-10/10, moderate to severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: ANOREXIA in >90% (ask 'are you hungry?' - 'yes' makes appendicitis unlikely), nausea and vomiting (AFTER pain onset), low-grade fever (100-102°F). KEY POSITIVES: Anorexia, right lower quadrant (RLQ) tenderness, periumbilical→RLQ migration. KEY NEGATIVES: Pain starting in right lower quadrant (RLQ) without migration is less typical. CRAMPY pain suggests obstruction. PROFUSE WATERY DIARRHEA suggests gastroenteritis. High fever >103°F suggests perforation.

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, acute, hours, 6-48 hours
  Negative indicators: chronic, months, years, gradual

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: right lower quadrant, RLQ, McBurney point, periumbilical migration
  Negative indicators: left side, LLQ, upper abdomen, diffuse
  Anatomical type: right_only

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: constant, hours, days, persistent
  Negative indicators: intermittent, episodic, minutes

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, stabbing, constant, severe
  Negative indicators: crampy, burning, dull, pressure-like

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: movement, coughing, walking, jumping
  Negative indicators: eating, rest, lying still

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: lying still, fetal position
  Negative indicators: movement, activity

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: constant, progressive, worsening
  Negative indicators: intermittent, comes and goes, episodic

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: severe, 7, 8, 9, 10, 7-10/10
  Negative indicators: mild, 1, 2, 3

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sudden severe pain that then improves briefly - may indicate PERFORATION

  🚨 Severe pain with abdominal rigidity (board-like abdomen) - perforation with peritonitis, call 911

  🚨 High fever >103°F with severe pain - possible perforation

  🚨 Hypotension, tachycardia, altered mental status - septic shock, call 911
