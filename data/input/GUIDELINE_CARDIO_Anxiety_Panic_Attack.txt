--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • chest pain
  • can't breathe
  • hyperventilating
  • panic attack

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Sudden, often with anxiety or stress trigger. LOCATION: Chest (variable location, can be diffuse or left-sided). DURATION: Minutes to hours, typically peaks at 10 minutes. CHARACTER: Sharp or vague discomfort, "can't catch breath", chest tightness. AGGRAVATING: Anxiety, stress, hyperventilation. RELIEVING: Reassurance, breathing exercises, anxiolytics. TIMING: Episodic, associated with stress/anxiety. SEVERITY: 5-8/10 subjectively but benign objectively. KEY POSITIVES: Hyperventilation, perioral tingling, carpopedal spasm, sense of impending doom, young patient, history of anxiety disorder, dyspnea out of proportion to findings. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT exertional pattern, NOT cardiac risk factors typically, NOT reproducible with palpation.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: sudden, often with anxiety, stress trigger
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: chest, variable location, can be diffuse, left-sided
  Negative indicators: exertional pattern, cardiac risk factors, reproducible with palpation

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: minutes to hours, typically peaks at 10 minutes, variable
  Negative indicators: continuous, unrelenting, days

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: sharp, vague discomfort, can't catch breath, chest tightness
  Negative indicators: exertional pattern, cardiac risk factors, reproducible with palpation

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: anxiety, stress, hyperventilation
  Negative indicators: reassurance, breathing exercises, anxiolytics

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: reassurance, breathing exercises, anxiolytics
  Negative indicators: anxiety, stress, hyperventilation

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: episodic, associated with stress, associated with anxiety
  Negative indicators: constant, unrelenting, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: 5-8/10, 5, 6, 7, 8, subjectively but benign objectively
  Negative indicators: mild, 1-4/10, severe, 9-10/10

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Cardiac risk factors present (must rule out cardiac)

  🚨 Exertional pattern (suggests cardiac)

  🚨 Older age (suggests cardiac)

  🚨 Diaphoresis (suggests cardiac)

  🚨 Radiation to arm/jaw (suggests cardiac)

  🚨 Syncope

  🚨 First episode ever in older patient
