CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • abdominal pain
  • belly pain
  • right lower abdominal pain
  • lower right side pain
  • severe constipation
  • distended abdomen

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: ACUTE onset over hours, may have intermittent episodes if chronic. LOCATION: Right lower quadrant (RLQ) or periumbilical, may become diffuse. DURATION: CONSTANT pain lasting hours. CHARACTER: SHARP, SEVERE, COLICKY pain initially, becoming CONSTANT if ischemia develops. AGGRAVATING: Eating or drinking worsens symptoms. RELIEVING: NOTHING relieves pain. TIMING: Initially INTERMITTENT crampy pain, becomes CONSTANT as ischemia progresses. SEVERITY: 7-10/10, severe. ASSOCIATED SYMPTOMS: Severe abdominal distension (dramatic), obstipation (no flatus or stool), nausea and vomiting, tympanic distended abdomen. History of chronic constipation or prior abdominal surgery. KEY POSITIVES: Massive abdominal distension, right lower quadrant (RLQ) tenderness, obstipation, sudden onset. KEY NEGATIVES: Ability to pass flatus or stool makes complete volvulus unlikely.

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Sudden severe pain with peritonitis - bowel necrosis/perforation, call 911

  🚨 Fever with severe distension - ischemic bowel

  🚨 Hypotension or shock - perforation or sepsis, call 911

  🚨 Bloody diarrhea - mucosal ischemia
