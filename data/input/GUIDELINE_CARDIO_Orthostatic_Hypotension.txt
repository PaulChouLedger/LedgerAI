CHIEF COMPLAINT TRIGGERS:
This diagnosis should be considered when patient presents with:
  • lightheadedness when standing
  • dizziness when standing up
  • feeling faint when standing

CLASSIC PRESENTATION:
ONSET: Immediate, within seconds to minutes of standing. LOCATION: Generalized lightheadedness, dizziness. DURATION: Brief, seconds to minutes, resolves when sitting/lying down. CHARACTER: Lightheadedness, dizziness, blurred vision, weakness. AGGRAVATING: STANDING UP from sitting or lying, prolonged standing, hot environment, dehydration. RELIEVING: Sitting or lying down immediately. TIMING: Reproducible with postural changes. SEVERITY: Variable, can lead to syncope if severe. KEY POSITIVES: Occurs with postural change, elderly patient, medications (antihypertensives, diuretics), dehydration, rapid resolution when sitting. KEY NEGATIVES: NOT constant dizziness, NOT spinning vertigo (BPPV), NOT exertional.

TYPICAL PATIENT DEMOGRAPHICS:
N/A

STRUCTURED OLDCARTS ASSESSMENT:
Use this structured data for dynamic questioning:

ONSET:
  Positive indicators: immediate, within seconds to minutes of standing, sudden
  Negative indicators: gradual, chronic, slow onset

LOCATION:
  Positive indicators: generalized lightheadedness, dizziness
  Negative indicators: localized, one area only, chest

DURATION:
  Positive indicators: brief, seconds to minutes, resolves when sitting lying down
  Negative indicators: continuous, unrelenting, hours

CHARACTER:
  Positive indicators: lightheadedness, dizziness, blurred vision, weakness
  Negative indicators: constant dizziness, spinning vertigo, exertional, chest pain

AGGRAVATING:
  Positive indicators: standing up from sitting, standing up from lying, prolonged standing, hot environment, dehydration
  Negative indicators: sitting, lying down, rest, medication

RELIEVING:
  Positive indicators: sitting, lying down immediately, rest
  Negative indicators: standing up, prolonged standing, hot environment

TIMING:
  Positive indicators: reproducible with postural changes, consistent
  Negative indicators: unpredictable, random, continuous

SEVERITY:
  Positive indicators: variable, can lead to syncope if severe, variable symptoms
  Negative indicators: mild, moderate, constant

⚠️  EMERGENCY WARNING SIGNS / RED FLAGS:
If patient reports any of the following, urgent action required:

  🚨 Syncope or actual loss of consciousness

  🚨 Falls or injury from lightheadedness

  🚨 Severe dehydration

  🚨 GI bleeding or anemia

  🚨 New medication (antihypertensive)

  🚨 Autonomic neuropathy (diabetes)
