# Cardiovascular Guidelines (35 Conditions)

## Overview

Evidence-based cardiovascular diagnostic guidelines following AHA (American Heart Association) and ER society standards.

## Urgency Classification

### Emergent (Life-Threatening) - 9 conditions
Require immediate intervention:
1. **Acute MI (STEMI)** - Heart attack with ST elevation
2. **Unstable Angina** - Rest angina or crescendo pattern
3. **Aortic Dissection** - Tearing chest/back pain
4. **Acute Aortic Syndrome** - Similar to dissection
5. **Pulmonary Embolism** - Blood clot in lung
6. **Acute Heart Failure** - Pulmonary edema
7. **Hypertensive Emergency** - BP >180/120 with end-organ damage
8. **Cardiac Tamponade** - Fluid compressing heart
9. **Ventricular Tachycardia** - Malignant arrhythmia
10. **NSTEMI** - Heart attack without ST elevation
11. **Cardiogenic Shock** - Heart failure with shock
12. **Pneumothorax** - Collapsed lung

### Urgent (Needs Prompt Attention) - 11 conditions
Require evaluation within hours:
1. **Pericarditis** - Inflamed heart sac
2. **Myocarditis** - Inflamed heart muscle
3. **SVT** - Supraventricular tachycardia
4. **Atrial Fibrillation** - Irregular rapid rhythm
5. **Atrial Flutter** - Regular rapid rhythm
6. **Endocarditis** - Heart valve infection
7. **Pericardial Effusion** - Fluid around heart (non-tamponade)
8. **Aortic Stenosis** - Narrowed aortic valve
9. **Bradycardia** - Slow heart rate with symptoms
10. **Takotsubo Cardiomyopathy** - Stress-induced cardiomyopathy
11. **Dilated Cardiomyopathy** - Weakened heart muscle

### Routine (Non-Urgent) - 13 conditions
Can be managed outpatient:
1. **Stable Angina** - Predictable exertional chest pain
2. **Hypertensive Urgency** - Elevated BP without symptoms
3. **Vasovagal Syncope** - Fainting
4. **Orthostatic Hypotension** - Lightheadedness when standing
5. **Benign Arrhythmia (PAC/PVC)** - Skipped beats
6. **Costochondritis** - Chest wall inflammation
7. **GERD** - Reflux mimicking cardiac pain
8. **Anxiety/Panic Attack** - Anxiety-related chest pain
9. **Prinzmetal Angina** - Vasospastic angina
10. **Mitral Valve Prolapse** - MVP with symptoms
11. **Esophageal Spasm** - Swallowing-related chest pain
12. **Pleurisy** - Pleural inflammation
13. **Pneumothorax** - (Can be routine if small)

## Key Differentiators

### Chief Complaint: "Chest Pain"

**Cardiac (Ischemic)**:
- Exertional pattern → Stable Angina
- At rest → Unstable Angina, NSTEMI
- Crushing/pressure → Acute MI
- Radiates to arm/jaw → MI, Unstable Angina

**Pleuritic (Worse with Breathing)**:
- Positional relief → Pericarditis
- Unilateral → Pneumothorax, Pleurisy, PE
- Reproducible with palpation → Costochondritis

**Tearing/Ripping**:
- Sudden maximal → Aortic Dissection
- Back pain → Aortic Dissection, Acute Aortic Syndrome

**Burning**:
- Post-meal → GERD
- Epigastric → GERD, Esophageal Spasm

### Chief Complaint: "Palpitations"

**Regular Rapid**:
- Sudden on/off → SVT
- Gradual → Atrial Flutter
- Exertional syncope → Aortic Stenosis

**Irregular**:
- Irregularly irregular → Atrial Fibrillation
- Brief skips → Benign Arrhythmia (PAC/PVC)

**Wide Complex**:
- Rapid with syncope → Ventricular Tachycardia

### Chief Complaint: "Shortness of Breath"

**Acute**:
- With leg swelling → PE
- Sudden pleuritic → Pneumothorax
- Pink frothy sputum → Acute Heart Failure

**Subacute**:
- Orthopnea/PND → Heart Failure, Dilated Cardiomyopathy
- With chest pain → PE, MI

### Chief Complaint: "Syncope"

**Exertional**:
- With murmur → Aortic Stenosis
- Young athlete → HCM, Arrhythmia

**Positional**:
- Standing → Orthostatic Hypotension
- With prodrome → Vasovagal Syncope

**Sudden (No Warning)**:
- Arrhythmia → VT, Heart Block
